Oil Surges Past $97 as Hormuz Closure Grips Markets
OilPrice / Rigzone / CNBC

The moves signal the Gulf's rapid pivot toward alternative export infrastructure and bilateral energy security arrangements.

Market Outlook
The Brent-WTI spread blowout — Brent at $108.90 versus WTI at $100.34, an $8.56 differential — is the defining market structure of this session and reflects two distinct pricing regimes: a geopolitical risk premium dominating Brent, and demand destruction anxiety anchoring WTI. The Hormuz closure remains the single largest supply risk in the market; with roughly 20-21 million barrels per day transiting the strait under normal conditions, even a partial or extended disruption justifies the $7-plus weekly gain in WTI futures through Thursday. The UAE's acceleration of its West-East bypass pipeline and the bilateral LNG and SPR deals with India are significant medium-term signals — Gulf producers are actively re-architecting export infrastructure, which could structurally reduce Hormuz leverage over time, but offers no near-term relief. The EIA May STEO will be closely scrutinized for any upward revisions to price forecasts and downward revisions to demand, which would validate the demand destruction argument that has kept WTI from clearing $105. The WTI futures curve is in steep backwardation — Jun 2026 at $104.78 rolling down sharply to Oct 2026 at $87.81 — signaling the market is pricing in resolution and supply normalization by late 2026, but that thesis is entirely contingent on a diplomatic outcome from the Trump-Xi summit. Key risks to the upside: Hormuz closure extends beyond 30 days or escalates to direct tanker interdiction; risks to the downside: coordinated SPR releases from IEA members, a credible Hormuz reopening deal, or a demand-side shock from recession signals in US or Chinese macro data.
