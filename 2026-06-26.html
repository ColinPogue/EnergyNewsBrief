Qatar Restarts Crude Loadings and LNG Exports After Iran War Disruption
OilPrice
QatarEnergy has issued its first crude tender since the Iran war began on February 28, offering Marine Qatar, Marine Land, and al-Shaheen grades for July and August loading via ship-to-ship transfers outside the Strait of Hormuz. Simultaneously, up to eight empty LNG carriers have massed at Ras Laffan and are set to load in coming days, with additional tankers en route, signaling a meaningful recovery in Qatari energy exports. The dual resumption of crude and LNG flows represents a key bullish supply signal for both commodity markets.
Natural Gas Futures Rebound as Heat Outlook Overrides Bearish Storage Print
NGI
Henry Hub natural gas futures reversed an early sell-off Thursday after a larger-than-expected storage injection, with traders pivoting to forecasts of widespread heat across demand regions next week. July and August contracts both settled higher, while cash prices strengthened across Gulf Coast hubs driven by robust LNG export demand and building Southern heat, offsetting weather-related weakness in the West. The price action underscores the market's sensitivity to near-term weather signals relative to weekly supply data.

Market Outlook
WTI's decisive breakdown below $70 is the defining price event of the week, and the technical and fundamental picture now tilts bearish into the near term. The collapse of the Hormuz disruption premium — the dominant bullish narrative for the past several weeks — removes a key support pillar, and the contango structure from Aug ($69.08) through Nov ($67.98) confirms the market is pricing in no meaningful supply tightness ahead. Qatar's restart of crude loadings in Marine, Land, and al-Shaheen grades for July-August adds physical supply at the margin, compounding the bearish tone even as the geopolitical backdrop remains fragile following Iran's strike on a commercial vessel near Oman. On the demand side, U.S. refinery runs at 96.1% capacity and 17.1 million b/d throughput are constructive, but with gasoline production at 9.5 million b/d and summer driving season largely priced in, this data point is insufficient to reverse the broader bearish momentum. The key risk to the downside view is a rapid re-escalation in the Strait of Hormuz — any further Iranian interdiction of commercial vessels or a breakdown in diplomatic channels could snap back 3-5% of geopolitical premium overnight. Traders should watch Brent's $72.00 level and WTI's $67.50 zone as the next critical tests; a weekly close below those marks would likely trigger systematic selling and a re-pricing toward the mid-$60s.
