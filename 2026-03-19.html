Iran Strikes Qatar's Ras Laffan, Oil Hits $119, Gas Surges 35%
OilPrice / NGI / CNBC / Upstream Online
Iranian missile strikes on Qatar's Ras Laffan Industrial City — home to the world's largest LNG liquefaction complex — forced a QatarEnergy evacuation and sent shockwaves through global energy markets. Brent crude briefly surged to $119/bbl while the Brent-WTI spread widened to an 11-year high of roughly $18/bbl, and European TTF gas futures opened 35% higher before settling at a 24% gain. Analysts at Kpler warn prices could reach $150/bbl if the conflict persists through end of March.
Trump Demands Energy Sites Spared as Middle East War Escalates
Rigzone / Upstream Online
President Trump called for energy infrastructure to be protected from attack as the Israel-Iran conflict escalated with strikes on key oil and gas facilities across the region, including South Pars and Ras Laffan. The appeal came amid mounting concern that prolonged warfare could cause lasting structural damage to Gulf energy infrastructure underpinning global supply. Upstream Online is tracking the broader US-Iran conflict with live coverage as the situation develops.
Kuwaiti Refinery Hit by Drone Attack Amid Gulf Conflict
Upstream Online
A Kuwaiti refinery has been attacked by drones, marking a significant escalation of the Middle East conflict beyond Iran and Qatar to Gulf Cooperation Council infrastructure. The strike raises fresh concerns about the vulnerability of downstream processing capacity in the region and the potential for broader supply chain disruption. Traders will be watching for any impact on crude throughput and regional product markets.
EIA: U.S. Natural Gas Consumption Hit Record 92 Bcf/d in 2025
EIA
U.S. natural gas consumption averaged a record 92.0 Bcf/d in 2025, up 2% from 2024, with January 2025 setting a new winter monthly high of 126.6 Bcf/d — a 5% year-on-year increase. The data, from EIA's Natural Gas Monthly, underscores sustained structural demand growth driven by power generation, LNG exports, and industrial use. The record baseline consumption figure adds context to current supply risk as Middle East disruptions threaten global LNG availability.
Spot Prices Wrap: Warming East Weighs on Natural Gas as Western Heat Drives Modest Gains
NGI
Spot natural gas prices lost ground Wednesday as warmer forecasts for the eastern half of the country would reduce heating demand, outweighing modest gains from a strengthening heatwave in the West.
Market Outlook
The Iranian missile strikes on Ras Laffan represent the most significant supply-side shock to global LNG markets in years, and despite Brent's dramatic reversal from $119 back toward $107, the risk premium is far from fully unwound — Kpler's $150/bbl scenario remains live if the conflict persists into April. The Brent-WTI spread at an 11-year high of ~$18 is the clearest expression of geopolitical dislocation in the market, and traders should watch this spread for early signals of whether Middle East risk is widening or compressing. The drone strike on a Kuwaiti refinery marks a critical escalation: if downstream processing infrastructure across the GCC becomes a sustained target, the market faces not just upstream supply risk but a regional product supply crunch that US shale cannot easily offset. On natural gas, the $3.17 Henry Hub print is likely understating the true global supply shock — European TTF's 24% settlement gain reflects how exposed LNG-dependent importers are, and with US consumption at a record 92 Bcf/d structural baseline, any incremental LNG export disruption tightens the domestic supply-demand balance faster than seasonal weather patterns can offset. US storage data, currently unavailable, is a critical missing piece — with record consumption and export disruption risk, any below-consensus injection or draw figure could amplify the NG move significantly. Key watch points: Trump's diplomatic push to protect energy infrastructure, any QatarEnergy operational update on Ras Laffan restart timelines, and whether the Kuwait strike triggers further GCC facility evacuations — the next 48 hours of geopolitical newsflow will set the direction for both crude and gas through month-end.
