Oil Plunges 19-20% in May on US-Iran Ceasefire Hopes
OilPrice / CNBC / Rigzone / Upstream Online
Brent crude is on track for its steepest monthly decline since 2020, falling roughly 19-20% in May as traders aggressively unwound geopolitical risk premiums following a tentative US-Iran agreement to extend their ceasefire by 60 days and pursue further nuclear talks. July WTI posted its largest weekly loss in months, sliding to $88.60 after trading as high as $94.70 earlier in the week, as market participants bet on diplomacy eventually reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Exxon and Chevron Warn of $150+ Oil as Inventories Crater
CNBC / Financial Times
Exxon executive Neil Chapman warned that physical Brent crude prices could spike to $150-$160 per barrel within weeks as global inventories approach all-time lows due to the Strait of Hormuz disruption, while Chevron's CEO separately cautioned that prices will jump sharply over the summer as the supply shock depletes market buffers. The Hormuz blockade has stripped up to 13 million barrels per day from global supply, exhausting what executives describe as the market's critical shock absorbers. The warnings stand in stark contrast to the current futures selloff driven by ceasefire optimism, highlighting a deepening divide between physical and paper markets.

Norway Pushes EU to Drop Arctic Drilling Moratorium
Rigzone

Market Outlook
The dominant market narrative remains the deepening schism between physical reality and paper market pricing: futures are selling off hard on US-Iran ceasefire optimism, yet the Hormuz blockade — the largest physical supply disruption in recorded history at up to 13 million bpd — continues to drain global inventories toward all-time lows. Brent's 19-20% May collapse and WTI's slide from $94.70 to sub-$88 this week reflect aggressive risk premium liquidation, but Exxon's Neil Chapman and Chevron's CEO are sounding the alarm that $150-$160 Brent is a realistic near-term scenario if diplomacy fails and inventories continue to crater. Traders should treat the current selloff with caution — the contango structure in WTI (Jul $87.93, Oct $81.81) implies market consensus around supply recovery, but that consensus is entirely contingent on a ceasefire holding and Hormuz eventually reopening, neither of which is guaranteed. On the gas side, the fundamental setup is constructively bullish: record Mexico import demand at 8.25 Bcf/d, peak summer cooling load incoming, and EIA projecting near-record power sector consumption this summer with a true record targeted for 2027 — all of which supports holding long NG exposure into the heat season. Key macro risk to watch: if US-Iran talks break down or the ceasefire lapses, expect a violent crude short squeeze that could see Brent reclaim $100+ within days, catching a heavily net-short futures market badly offside. Additionally, Norway's push to lift the EU Arctic drilling moratorium bears watching as a medium-term supply signal for European markets, though meaningful production from Barents Sea development remains years away.
