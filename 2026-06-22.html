Top Stories
Iran Hormuz Crisis: Closure Claims, Stalled Talks, Oil Swings
CNBC / World Oil / Upstream Online / Rigzone
Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz closed again, citing Lebanese ceasefire violations tied to continued Israeli strikes, while the U.S. maintained the waterway remains open and vessel tracking confirmed oil continued flowing through the strait. Shipping traffic slowed and oil prices whipsawed as U.S.-Iran peace talks stalled, though Qatar and Pakistan announced a 60-day diplomatic roadmap and Iranian tankers were observed continuing to transit the strait. Traders face acute uncertainty: any confirmed closure of Hormuz would threaten roughly 20% of globally traded crude and LNG flows.
Explosion at Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG Hub Injures 54, 18 Missing
OilPrice / Upstream Online
An explosion and fire struck QatarEnergy's Ras Laffan Industrial City during start-up operations at the Barzan local gas facility, injuring more than 54 people and leaving 18 unaccounted for as of early Monday. The incident at one of the world's most critical LNG export hubs raises immediate concerns about supply disruptions for Asian and European buyers dependent on Qatari cargoes. Traders should monitor QatarEnergy's operational updates closely as any extended outage at Ras Laffan could tighten global LNG spot markets significantly.
Natural Gas Futures Push Higher as Storage Deficit Widens
NGI
July natural gas futures settled higher Thursday after the EIA storage report showed injections continuing to lag year-earlier levels, extending a season-long trend that is steadily eroding the storage cushion. Cash prices softened into the Juneteenth holiday weekend on weaker power demand forecasts, while Permian Basin forward hubs rebounded sharply during the June 10–17 period even as New England winter strip prices pulled back. Traders are balancing robust production growth against tightening storage margins and an uncertain summer weather outlook.
Hanwha Ocean Joins $15.7 Billion Canada FLNG Project
Upstream Online
South Korean shipbuilder Hanwha Ocean has signed on to a proposed $15.7 billion floating LNG project in Canada, adding a major engineering and construction partner to what would be one of the largest FLNG developments in the Western Hemisphere. The project signals continued appetite for long-cycle LNG infrastructure investment despite near-term price volatility driven by the Iran conflict and shifting demand patterns. For LNG traders, Canadian FLNG capacity additions would diversify Atlantic basin supply and offer an alternative export route to Asian buyers.
Permian natural gas production increased faster than crude oil
EIA
The Permian region's marketed natural gas production grew from 17.2 billion cubic feet per day (Bcf/d) in 2021 to 27.6 Bcf/d in 2025, a 60% increase, according to data from our latest Short Term Energy Outlook. Over the same period, crude oil production grew by 39%, going from 4.7 million barrels per day (b/d) to 6.6 million b/d.
Market Outlook
Crude markets are caught in a high-frequency geopolitical crossfire: Iran's Hormuz closure claim — contested by U.S. officials and live vessel tracking — is generating violent intraday swings, with WTI printing a 1.76% loss to $75.25 even as physical flows remained intact. The key risk asymmetry here is severe — a confirmed, sustained Hormuz closure threatens roughly 20% of globally traded crude and LNG, representing a potential $10–$15/bbl shock scenario, while a diplomatic resolution via the Qatar-Pakistan 60-day roadmap could strip $4–$6 of geopolitical premium and push WTI toward the low $70s. Compounding the LNG supply picture, the explosion at Ras Laffan's Barzan facility — 54 injured, 18 missing — introduces immediate uncertainty for Qatari cargo commitments to Asian and European buyers; traders should treat any QatarEnergy production update as a market-moving event. On the natural gas side, the structural bull case is building quietly: the EIA storage deficit is widening on a year-over-year basis, Permian gas output has surged 60% to 27.6 Bcf/d since 2021 yet has not fully offset tightening margins, and the Ras Laffan incident could redirect LNG demand back toward U.S. export terminals. Baker Hughes rig count data was unavailable for this session, leaving a gap in the near-term U.S. supply trajectory read — that figure warrants close attention on next release. Key macro watch: U.S.-Iran nuclear talk progress, QatarEnergy operational bulletins out of Ras Laffan, and weekly EIA storage prints will be the three dominant price drivers heading into end of June.
