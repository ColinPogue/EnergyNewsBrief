Top Stories
OPEC Output Plunges 8 MMbpd as Iran War Disrupts Exports
World Oil / Upstream Online
OPEC crude production has fallen by nearly 8 million barrels per day as the U.S.-Iran conflict severely disrupts member-state exports, marking one of the most significant supply shocks in the cartel's history. The disruption is centered on Iran's curtailed output and chokepoint pressures at the Strait of Hormuz, rippling across global supply chains. Traders are watching closely for any OPEC emergency response or compensatory output increases from Gulf members.
BP Books Exceptional Q1 Trading Profit Amid Iran War Volatility
OilPrice / CNBC / Financial Times
BP has flagged an 'exceptional' oil trading result for Q1 2026, a sharp reversal from the weak fourth quarter of 2025, driven by extreme price volatility since the outbreak of the Middle East war. The energy shock has triggered intense competition among traders and refineries scrambling for available crude cargoes as Iranian supply tightens. BP and rival supermajors typically do not disclose exact trading figures, but the signal points to outsized desk profits across the sector.
Oil Slides as IEA Warns Demand Destruction Spreads, Iran Talks Revive
CNBC
Crude prices fell after the IEA cautioned that demand destruction from elevated prices is broadening globally, while U.S. Vice President JD Vance indicated fresh Iran peace negotiations are possible but depend on Tehran's next move. The dual pressure of weakening demand signals and diplomatic de-escalation hopes is tempering the war-risk premium that has dominated markets. Traders remain on alert as any ceasefire progress or Hormuz reopening could trigger sharp downside moves.
U.S. coal-fired generating capacity retired in 2025 was the least in 15 years
EIA
During 2025, the U.S. electric power sector retired 2.6 gigawatts (GW) of coal-fired generating capacity at four power plants, the least since 2010. At the beginning of 2025, coal plant operators had planned to retire 8.5 GW of capacity; however, 4.8 GW of planned retirements were delayed to a future year, and the operators of two coal plants (1.1 GW) cancelled plans to retire.

Market Outlook
The market is navigating a rare and violent collision of forces: an 8 MMbpd OPEC supply shock driven by U.S.-Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz pressure on one side, and accelerating IEA-flagged demand destruction plus ceasefire diplomacy on the other. WTI at $97.30 and Brent at $99.03 reflect this tug-of-war, with Brent holding closer to $100 on supply scarcity while WTI bears the brunt of macro demand pessimism. BP's flagged 'exceptional' Q1 trading profit confirms that volatility — not directionality — is the dominant trading environment, and rival supermajor desks are likely posting similar outsized results as refineries scramble for non-Iranian barrels. The critical near-term risk is ceasefire or Hormuz reopening news: any credible de-escalation signal from the JD Vance-Iran diplomatic channel could trigger a $5-$8 downside flush in Brent as war premium unwinds rapidly. Conversely, if OPEC fails to announce emergency compensatory output from Saudi Arabia or the UAE in the coming sessions, the supply gap of 8 MMbpd will reassert upward pressure and likely push Brent through $100 with conviction. Traders should also keep the coal retirement story on the radar — with 4.8 GW of planned U.S. coal retirements delayed, natural gas demand upside from power switching is more limited than the bulls had priced in, keeping NatGas pinned in a tight range near $2.61.
