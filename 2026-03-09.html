Oil Tops $100 as Iran War Closes Strait of Hormuz
Rigzone / World Oil / CNBC
The U.S.-Israel war on Iran has effectively shut the Strait of Hormuz, triggering what Rapidan Energy calls the largest oil supply disruption in history and pushing crude prices above $100 per barrel. The lack of a coordinated drawdown decision leaves markets without a near-term supply-side buffer as Hormuz disruptions persist. Traders should note that a formal IEA release remains on the table and could cap price spikes if a consensus emerges in coming days.

Market Outlook
The Strait of Hormuz closure marks a structural supply shock with no historical precedent in scope, and with Brent already trading above $100 and WTI at $96.69, price discovery is now being driven by war risk premiums rather than traditional supply-demand fundamentals. Rapidan Energy's characterization of this as the largest oil supply disruption in history is not hyperbole — the strait handles an estimated 17–20 million barrels per day, and even a partial, sustained blockage will force demand destruction through price as the only balancing mechanism available. The G7's failure to reach an immediate IEA SPR release agreement is a critical gap: while 'necessary measures' were pledged, the absence of a coordinated drawdown leaves markets without a credible supply-side ceiling, and any formal IEA consensus — which remains on the table — would be the single most important bearish catalyst to monitor in the coming 48–72 hours. Saudi Arabia's decision to cut output as storage fills amid the near-blockage removes another potential relief lever, signaling that even traditional swing producers are operationally constrained by the disruption rather than positioned to offset it. On the natural gas side, the 67% weekly TTF spike and 30% Monday open gap represent a European energy crisis in formation, and while Henry Hub at $3.12 appears subdued by comparison, the redirection of LNG cargoes away from Asia and toward Europe will tighten U.S. export economics and could accelerate domestic price uplift if the conflict extends beyond 2–3 weeks. Key risks to the upside include an escalation of tanker incidents in the Hormuz corridor or Iranian missile activity targeting Gulf infrastructure; key risks to the downside include a ceasefire signal, a coordinated IEA release of 60–100 million barrels, or diplomatic de-escalation that reopens transit lanes — any of which could trigger a $5–8 intraday reversal in WTI.
