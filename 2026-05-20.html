Top Stories
Hormuz Slowly Reopens: Supertankers Exit, India Eyes Direct Loading
OilPrice / Upstream Online
Two supertankers carrying Iraqi and Qatari crude cleared the Strait of Hormuz and are heading to China, ordered by Unipec and Sinochem — the first confirmed transits after Iran reportedly allowed up to 30 vessels through last week. Separately, India is preparing to send empty tankers into the Strait to load crude and LPG directly from Gulf producers, the first such move in roughly 80 days since the Iran war began disrupting the chokepoint. These developments signal a tentative, partial reopening of the world's most critical oil shipping lane, though the situation remains highly fluid.
Oil Dips as Trump Delays Iran Strike on Regional Leaders' Request
CNBC
Crude prices edged lower after President Trump announced he would postpone a planned military strike on Iran following diplomatic appeals from key Middle Eastern leaders, injecting fresh uncertainty over the trajectory of the US-Iran conflict. Traders are weighing mixed signals from Washington as any de-escalation path could ease the Hormuz disruption premium baked into current prices. The Adani settlement with US Treasury, which involved purchases of sanctioned Iranian energy between late 2023 and mid-2025, adds further complexity to the sanctions enforcement picture.
UK Loosens Russian Sanctions, Allows Diesel and Jet Fuel Imports
Rigzone / Financial Times
The UK government will permit imports of diesel and jet fuel refined from Russian crude in third countries such as India and Turkey, effectively easing a key sanctions restriction to alleviate supply tightness caused by the ongoing Iran war. The move mirrors similar relief measures adopted in the United States and is designed to address refined product supply gaps rather than directly benefit Russian crude revenues. The policy shift is significant for Atlantic Basin fuel markets and may influence refined product spreads.
US Industrial Gas Demand to Hit Records in 2026 and 2027: EIA
EIA / NGI
EIA's latest Short-Term Energy Outlook projects US industrial natural gas consumption will set successive record highs in 2026 and 2027, after already reaching a record 23.6 Bcf/d in 2025. Growth is driven by expansion in natural gas-weighted manufacturing sectors, underpinning a structurally bullish demand floor for the domestic gas market. Henry Hub spot prices were already climbing on Wednesday on record-challenging East Coast heat, per NGI, reinforcing the near-term tightness narrative.
Spot Natural Gas Prices Extend Gains as Henry Hub Climbs, Northeast Slides
NGI
Natural gas spot prices rallied broadly for Wednesday delivery as record-challenging heat held over the East Coast, though Northeast hubs slumped with cooler Canadian air pushing toward New England.
Market Outlook
WTI's 5% single-session drop to $102.31 is the dominant story, but traders should resist reading it as a clean de-escalation trade — the Hormuz reopening remains partial, fragile, and diplomatically contingent, with only two confirmed supertanker transits and India's direct-loading move still in preparation phase after roughly 80 days of disruption. Trump's delay of an Iran strike, reportedly at the request of regional leaders, introduces a new layer of uncertainty: the war premium does not disappear, it reprices to reflect optionality, and any breakdown in back-channel diplomacy could see $110+ WTI re-emerge rapidly. On the supply side, the UK's decision to permit Russian-origin diesel and jet fuel refined in third countries like India and Turkey mirrors US relief measures and will compress Atlantic Basin refined product spreads — a bearish signal for crack spreads that traders in the products market need to price. The Adani-US Treasury settlement, involving sanctioned Iranian crude purchases through mid-2025, is a secondary but non-trivial read on sanctions enforcement credibility; looser enforcement at the margin is modestly bearish for Iranian supply fears. Natural gas at $3.08 looks well-supported structurally — EIA's record 23.6 Bcf/d industrial demand in 2025 and projected highs in 2026-2027 provide a demand floor, and East Coast heat is providing near-term spot uplift even as Northeast hubs soften on Canadian air. Key watches: confirmation of sustained Hormuz transits beyond the initial 30-vessel authorization, any White House signaling on Iran strike rescheduling, Wednesday's delayed inventory data, and whether WTI can hold the $100 handle — a break there opens a fast technical move toward $97-$98.
