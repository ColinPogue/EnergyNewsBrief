Top Stories
Ukraine Drone Strike Sets Russian Baltic Oil Port Ablaze
Rigzone
Ukraine launched its most intense air strike in over a year, targeting Russia's Ust-Luga oil port on the Baltic Sea and setting it on fire. The attack raises immediate concerns about disruptions to Russian crude and petroleum product exports through the key Baltic terminal. Traders should monitor vessel diversions and any downstream impact on European supply flows.
Hormuz Crisis Tightens Grip: Iraq Tankers, Kuwait Warns, Italy Scrambles
World Oil / Upstream Online / Financial Times
Iraqi crude tanker AIS signals are being questioned as Strait of Hormuz transit conditions remain unclear, while Kuwait Petroleum's chief declared there is no viable replacement for the waterway as a global oil artery. Italy's Prime Minister Meloni is urgently seeking Algerian gas supplies to offset curtailed Qatari LNG shipments caused by the Iran war, underscoring how the conflict is reshaping energy supply chains from the Gulf to Europe. Collectively, these developments signal persistent, multi-front pressure on crude and LNG flows that traders cannot price away in the near term.
ConocoPhillips CEO: Iran War Locks In Natural Gas Price Upside
NGI
ConocoPhillips CEO Ryan Lance told investors that natural gas market 'headwinds have become tailwinds,' arguing that constrained global production means elevated prices are likely to persist regardless of how quickly the Iran conflict resolves. Front-month natural gas futures reflected that view, settling higher Tuesday on rebounding oil prices, lingering domestic demand, and expectations of at least one more storage withdrawal, even as back-end contracts softened on Iran peace-deal optimism. Cash markets, however, posted broad losses for Wednesday delivery as softer weather-driven demand weighed on most regions.
Iraq Revives Turkey Pipeline; India Eyes $20B Drilling Surge
Upstream Online / OilPrice / World Oil
Iraq is preparing its northern pipeline for a resumption of oil exports via Turkey, a route that has been offline and whose revival would add meaningful Kirkuk crude volumes back to Mediterranean markets. Separately, India's ONGC is planning an $18–$20 billion drilling campaign — the country's largest ever — to reduce import dependence, with rig tenders already issued, as New Delhi and Washington also held talks over Hormuz disruption and India's LNG supply strain. Together, these moves signal significant shifts in both supply routing and upstream investment driven directly by the ongoing Gulf crisis.

Market Outlook
The market is navigating a historically complex collision of bearish macro pressure and acute geopolitical supply risk, and today's price action — Brent -9.72%, WTI -5.73% — suggests that forced deleveraging and demand destruction fears are currently overwhelming the geopolitical risk premium. The Ukraine drone strike on Ust-Luga, one of Russia's most strategically important Baltic export terminals, would under normal circumstances be a sharp bullish catalyst; the fact that Brent still collapsed nearly $10 speaks to the severity of the macro headwinds and the crowded nature of long positioning heading into this session. Hormuz remains the dominant structural risk: Iraqi tanker AIS anomalies, Kuwait's unambiguous warning that there is no replacement for the strait, and Italy's emergency pivot to Algerian gas collectively confirm that Gulf supply chain disruption is not a tail risk but an active, multi-front constraint on global crude and LNG flows. The Iraq-Turkey northern pipeline revival is a meaningful offset to watch — Kirkuk volumes returning to Mediterranean markets would ease European crude tightness materially, but timelines remain uncertain and should not be traded as imminent. On natural gas, ConocoPhillips' structural bull case is credible over the medium term, but the near-term $2.85 print reflects the market's struggle to price geopolitical tailwinds against weak domestic weather demand and softening back-end contracts on Iran peace-deal optionality. Key watches for the next 48 hours: any confirmed disruption to Ust-Luga export flows, updated Hormuz transit data and vessel tracking out of the Iraqi terminals, US inventory figures when available, and whether WTI can reclaim $90 — failure to do so accelerates the bearish technical setup toward $85.
