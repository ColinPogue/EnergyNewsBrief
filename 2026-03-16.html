Top Stories
Strait of Hormuz Crisis: Coalition Falters, Iranian Tankers Still Moving
CNBC / Financial Times
President Trump admitted Monday that key U.S. allies are reluctant to join a naval coalition to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with some described as 'less than enthusiastic.' Treasury Secretary Bessent separately confirmed the U.S. is allowing Iranian oil tankers through the strait, with the White House tolerating the trade to avoid worsening supply shortages — a stance the Financial Times reports is earning Tehran a significant oil windfall.
Hormuz Closure Wipes 12 MMboepd; Asian Refiners Face Heavy Crude Crunch
OilPrice / Rigzone
More than 12 million boepd of Middle East supply has been taken offline since the Strait of Hormuz closure triggered by U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran, including 7 million bpd of crude — roughly 7% of global liquids demand. Iraq has been hit hardest, with over 60% of its production disrupted, leaving Asian refiners scrambling to replace lost Arab heavy and medium grades. Brent closed above $100 for a third consecutive session as supply fears remained elevated, even as prices eased slightly on the day.
IEA Releases Emergency Stocks as Birol Targets Hard-Hit Asian Buyers
Upstream Online / Rigzone / World Oil
The IEA confirmed it has additional emergency oil reserves available and is directing stocks toward Asian countries hardest hit by the Strait of Hormuz disruption, IEA chief Fatih Birol said. The release comes as shipping through the strait has ground to a near-halt, amplifying pressure on importers across Japan, South Korea, India, and China. TotalEnergies separately warned the Middle East conflict could cut as much as 15% of its own output, underscoring the breadth of exposure across major operators.

Market Outlook
The 400+ basis point intraday collapse in WTI to $94.67 is the market sending a clear message: the geopolitical premium is being stress-tested by coalition fractures and the White House's tacit tolerance of Iranian oil flows through the strait. Treasury Secretary Bessent's confirmation that Iranian tankers are transiting freely is a material bearish development — it implies the 7 MMbpd crude disruption figure may overstate net market tightness if sanctioned Iranian barrels are quietly backfilling Asian demand. That said, Brent's third consecutive close above $100 and the IEA's emergency stock deployment targeting Japan, South Korea, India, and China confirm the physical supply crunch is real, particularly for heavy and medium sour grades with no easy replacement. The key risk bifurcation here is geopolitical escalation versus diplomatic resolution: any hardening of the U.S. stance on Iranian tanker passage — or a coalition breakthrough — would send Brent back toward $105-$110, while a continued softening could collapse the risk premium and drag WTI toward the high $80s. TotalEnergies flagging up to 15% output exposure adds a corporate earnings and production guidance risk layer that institutional desks will begin pricing into Q3 outlooks. Next key catalysts to watch: IEA emergency release volume specifics, any NATO or Gulf ally response to the coalition request, weekly EIA crude inventory data, and whether the WTI-Brent spread continues to widen — a widening spread would signal U.S. market-specific bearish pressure decoupling from the global supply shock narrative.
