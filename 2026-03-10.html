Top Stories
Hormuz Crisis Sends Brent to $94 as Analysts Warn No Quick Fix
OilPrice / World Oil / Financial Times / CNBC
Brent crude settled at $94/barrel on March 9 — a roughly 50% surge since the start of the year — as the near-standstill in tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz and widespread infrastructure attacks reshape the global supply outlook. The EIA sharply revised its oil price forecast in its latest STEO, while analysts warn that even a swift end to the Iran war would not restore normal supply conditions quickly, as governments pivot to prioritizing energy security over cost efficiency. The IEA's member countries convened Tuesday to discuss a potential coordinated release of strategic petroleum reserves, though no decision had been reached as of midday.
Natural Gas Futures Slide as Warm Weather Caps Iran War Premium
NGI
Despite the geopolitical shock from the Iran war, April Henry Hub natural gas futures fell midday Tuesday as springlike temperatures and soft domestic demand drove expectations for modest storage withdrawals heading into injection season. The EIA's latest STEO revised the Henry Hub price forecast downward, signaling that ample Lower 48 supply and comfortable inventory levels are offsetting the war-related risk premium. Traders are watching whether a prolonged Hormuz disruption eventually tightens LNG feedgas demand enough to reverse the bearish domestic price trend.
Gulf Producers Deepen Output Cuts as Middle East Conflict Escalates
Rigzone
Major Gulf oil producers are deepening supply cuts amid the ongoing regional conflict, further tightening an already stressed global crude market at a time when Hormuz transit has nearly ground to a halt. The move adds a supply-side squeeze on top of the logistics disruptions already hammering tanker flows and refinery feedstock availability globally. For traders, the combination of deliberate OPEC-aligned cuts and physical infrastructure damage is compressing available barrels from the world's most critical export region simultaneously.

Market Outlook
Today's historic single-session selloff — Brent down 16.6%, WTI down 15.5% — represents one of the sharpest risk-premium unwinds in recent memory, but traders should not confuse price correction with fundamental resolution. The Hormuz disruption remains active, Gulf producers are deepening voluntary cuts, and the IEA has yet to pull the trigger on a coordinated SPR release, meaning the physical supply crunch is still very much in play even as paper markets reprice. Brent's retreat from $94 to $82.54 in a single session reeks of forced liquidation and momentum-driven selling rather than a clean macro reversal — watch for a dead-cat bounce attempt in the $82-$84 range if no further negative catalysts emerge overnight. On the bearish side, the 3% drop in U.S. crude exports in 2025 — the first annual decline since 2021 — signals softening global demand absorption and reduces the buffer that American barrels have historically provided in supply-shock environments. Natural gas at $2.98 is telling its own story: despite the most significant Middle East disruption in years, the domestic market simply doesn't care yet, though a prolonged Hormuz standstill tightening LNG feedgas demand could eventually flip that narrative hard into Q3. Key risks to monitor: any IEA SPR announcement could accelerate the crude selloff toward $78 WTI, while a re-escalation of infrastructure attacks or a Hormuz closure confirmation could snap prices back above $90 Brent within sessions — position sizing and stop discipline are critical in this volatility regime.
