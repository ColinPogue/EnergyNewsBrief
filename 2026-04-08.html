Top Stories
US-Iran Ceasefire Opens Hormuz; Oil Plunges Below $95
Financial Times / CNBC / World Oil / Upstream Online
A two-week US-Iran ceasefire announced by President Trump — following discussions with Pakistan's PM Sharif — prompted Iran to guarantee safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz, sending Brent and WTI crashing from triple-digit levels to below $95/bbl. The deal, based on a 10-point Iranian plan Trump called a 'workable basis' for negotiations, triggered a broad relief rally across risk assets globally. Traders are now watching whether the ceasefire holds and what it means for the roughly 20% of global oil supply that transits Hormuz.
China's Teapot Refiners Rush Iranian Crude as Prices Slide
OilPrice
China's independent 'teapot' refiners are aggressively seeking prompt Iranian crude cargoes after Beijing issued a fresh round of import quotas and international prices dropped sharply on ceasefire news. With Brent falling below $100/bbl, the discount on sanctioned Iranian barrels has narrowed but volume appetite among Chinese buyers remains strong. The buying surge underscores how quickly lower prices and quota availability translate into demand signals from the world's largest crude importer.
Eni Strikes 2 Tcf Gas Discovery Offshore Egypt
World Oil
Eni has made a significant 2 trillion cubic feet natural gas discovery offshore Egypt, with the company flagging fast-track development potential for the find. Fast-track development, if confirmed, would position the asset as a near-term contributor to regional supply.

Market Outlook
The US-Iran ceasefire is the most significant macro catalyst for oil markets since the February 28 Hormuz closure, and today's 16% WTI collapse to $94.51 reflects a near-complete unwind of the geopolitical risk premium accumulated over the past several weeks. The critical variable now is ceasefire durability: the deal is explicitly framed as a two-week arrangement based on a 10-point Iranian plan, meaning traders face a binary re-escalation risk on a known timeline — any sign of diplomatic breakdown before permanent terms are reached would likely drive a violent snapback toward the $105–$110 range. On the demand side, China's teapot refiners are already moving aggressively into discounted Iranian prompt barrels following Beijing's fresh quota issuance, which provides a near-term demand floor and suggests Chinese crude import volumes could surge in coming weeks if prices stabilize in the low-to-mid $90s. However, macro headwinds are mounting: Delta's decision to meaningfully cut growth plans in response to elevated fuel costs signals that demand destruction is already occurring at the airline level, and further pullbacks in aviation and industrial demand could accelerate if prices remain above $90 for an extended period. The Eni 2 Tcf Egypt discovery adds a medium-term bearish supply signal for global gas and LNG markets, though fast-track development timelines remain unconfirmed and near-term impact is limited. Key data to watch: confirmation of Iranian crude flows resuming through Hormuz, weekly US crude inventory prints once EIA data becomes available again, and any official statement from Tehran or Washington on the status of follow-on nuclear negotiations, which represent the only path to a durable supply normalization.
