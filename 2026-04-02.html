Top Stories
Trump Escalates Iran War as Hormuz Blockade Enters Second Month
Rigzone / OilPrice / Upstream Online / CNBC
President Trump threatened fresh strikes on Iran while claiming the war is 'very close' to completion, rattling global markets and sending stocks and bonds lower. Oil prices have surged more than 60% since the conflict began, with the Strait of Hormuz at a near-standstill for over a month, severely disrupting global energy flows. The UK is set to host talks with 35 nations on restoring Hormuz transits, while ADNOC's chief called Iran's actions 'global economic extortion' and urged coordinated international action.
Top Energy Traders Wrongfooted as Iran Crisis Defies Playbooks
Financial Times
The world's leading energy trading houses, firms that typically profit from volatility, were caught off guard by the speed and scale of the Iran war-driven energy shock, according to the Financial Times. The crisis has exposed the limits of standard hedging strategies as supply disruptions proved deeper and more sustained than most scenario models anticipated. Traders are now reassessing risk frameworks amid an oil market the FT describes as unlike any seen in recent decades.
Oil Demand Destruction Risk Grows as Iran War Drags On
CNBC / Upstream Online
CNBC analysts warn that Trump's timeline for ending the Iran conflict may not be fast enough to prevent lasting damage to global oil demand, as high prices and supply uncertainty begin curbing consumption. Oil has already surged over 60% in March alone, pushing fuel costs to levels that are altering consumer behavior and industrial activity worldwide.
India's LPG Crisis Deepens as Hormuz Cuts Off 90% of Imports
OilPrice
India is fast-tracking expansion of its piped natural gas network for household use after the Strait of Hormuz blockade choked off roughly 90% of the country's LPG import route, hitting the 60% of Indian households that rely on LPG for cooking. The supply crunch is forcing a structural energy pivot that could accelerate long-term domestic gas infrastructure investment across South Asia. Traders should watch for redirected LNG and LPG cargoes as India scrambles to secure alternative supply chains.
U.S. Crude Output Hits Record 13.6 Million B/D in 2025
EIA
U.S. crude oil production grew 3%, or 350,000 b/d, in 2025 to set a new annual record of 13.6 million b/d, according to EIA's latest Short-Term Energy Outlook. The Lower 48 states excluding the Gulf of America contributed 11.3 million b/d, representing 83% of total domestic output. The record comes as geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East have dramatically tightened global supply, adding strategic weight to rising U.S. production capacity.
Market Outlook
The market is now pricing in a scenario that most hedging models were never designed for: a sustained, militarily enforced blockade of the world's most critical oil chokepoint entering its second month with no clear off-ramp. WTI at $108.44 and Brent at $109.24 represent a 60%-plus rally since the Iran conflict ignited, and the FT's reporting that top trading houses were wrongfooted underscores how deeply this crisis has broken from historical playbooks. The primary upside risk remains an escalation in Trump's threatened strikes on Iran, which could push WTI through $115 and toward the $120 psychological threshold if Hormuz transits deteriorate further. On the demand side, CNBC's demand destruction warning is the key downside risk to monitor — at $108+ WTI, industrial consumption curtailments and fuel substitution are already beginning, and a sustained period above $110 could meaningfully erode the demand base that is currently supporting prices. U.S. record output of 13.6 million b/d at least provides some buffer and limits the upside for U.S. differentials, but it cannot compensate for the 20%+ of global seaborne crude that transits Hormuz. Traders should watch the outcome of the UK-hosted 35-nation talks on Hormuz as the single most important near-term catalyst — any credible de-escalation framework could trigger a $10-15 reversal in crude almost immediately, while failure to produce a unified response would likely send Brent through $115 before month-end.
