EU Eyes Energy Tax Cuts to Cushion Hormuz Crisis Blow
OilPrice
The European Commission is set to release a policy paper next week recommending lower energy taxes and reduced grid charges to offset the economic damage of the Hormuz crisis on European households and industry. Several EU member states have already enacted uncoordinated national relief measures while awaiting a unified Brussels response. The move signals how rapidly the Hormuz disruption is translating into political and fiscal pressure across importing nations.
U.S. coal-fired generating capacity retired in 2025 was the least in 15 years
EIA
During 2025, the U.S. electric power sector retired 2.6 gigawatts (GW) of coal-fired generating capacity at four power plants, the least since 2010. At the beginning of 2025, coal plant operators had planned to retire 8.5 GW of capacity; however, 4.8 GW of planned retirements were delayed to a future year, and the operators of two coal plants (1.1 GW) cancelled plans to retire.

Market Outlook
The U.S. naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz is the single most consequential supply-side shock the oil market has faced since the 1973 Arab oil embargo, and today's +6% surge in both WTI ($102.48) and Brent ($101.02) almost certainly underprices the tail risk if the standoff hardens. Roughly 20 million barrels per day of crude and refined products — approximately 20% of global seaborne oil trade — flow through Hormuz, and even a partial disruption would overwhelm any SPR release or OPEC spare capacity response in the near term. The Financial Times warning that the crisis is 'still in its early stages' deserves serious weight: Iran holds the geographic and asymmetric military advantage in the strait, and political pressure is already metastasizing into EU fiscal policy discussions around emergency energy tax cuts, signaling that importing nations are bracing for a prolonged disruption. Key upside risks include Iranian mining of the strait, retaliatory strikes on Gulf infrastructure, or a breakdown in any back-channel diplomacy; the brief stabilization noted in today's session on 'deal optimism' is fragile and should not be read as resolution. On the supply structure side, the Baker Hughes rig count data was unavailable today, limiting visibility into whether U.S. producers are signaling a drilling response to triple-digit prices — this data point becomes critical next week. Traders should maintain long crude exposure with tight stops below $100 WTI, watch for any State Department or Iranian Foreign Ministry statements after market hours, and treat the coal retirement slowdown story as a secondary bullish signal for domestic power-sector energy demand resilience heading into summer.
