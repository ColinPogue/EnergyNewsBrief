Top Stories
Oil Hits $110 as Iran Strait Tensions Persist Despite Trump Pause
CNBC / NGI / EIA
Crude prices climbed to $110 per barrel after Trump's five-day pause on strikes against Iran's energy infrastructure failed to fully ease supply fears, even as Iran allowed 10 oil tankers through the Strait of Hormuz. A larger-than-expected U.S. natural gas storage withdrawal and the looming expiration of Trump's ceasefire deadline also lifted natural gas futures across the curve. EIA data confirmed VLCC tanker rates out of the Middle East to Asia hit multi-decade highs following Iran's March 2 Hormuz closure.
India Slashes Fuel Taxes, Curbs Exports Amid Iran War Crisis
OilPrice / Rigzone / CNBC
India cut domestic excise duties on petrol and diesel by 10 rupees per liter each while simultaneously imposing a levy on fuel exports, absorbing a significant hit to tax revenues to protect consumers from soaring oil prices driven by the Iran conflict. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman confirmed the measures, framing them as a direct response to the West Asia crisis disrupting global energy supply. The dual policy move signals how severely the Iran war is rippling through major Asian oil-importing economies.
Middle East Energy Repairs to Cost $25 Billion; War Reshapes Security Strategy
Upstream Online / Financial Times
Damage to Middle Eastern energy infrastructure from the ongoing conflict is estimated to cost at least $25 billion to repair, according to a new assessment, highlighting the long-term structural impact on regional supply capacity. Baker Hughes CEO told Upstream Online the war will intensify global momentum toward energy security and supply diversification, accelerating investment in alternative supply chains. Global shipping fuel costs have risen nearly $5 billion since the conflict began, as vessels forgo cargo capacity to carry extra fuel for longer rerouting voyages.
Trump Policy Volatility Unnerves Energy Industry Despite Regulatory Relief
Financial Times / World Oil
Energy executives say Donald Trump's unpredictable policy swings are creating instability for the industry, even as they welcome his rollback of green regulations and permitting requirements, according to the Financial Times. Interior Secretary Doug Burgum pushed back, telling World Oil that the administration's domestic energy agenda remains on track and proceeding as planned. The tension between regulatory tailwinds and geopolitical unpredictability is complicating long-term investment planning across the sector.
Middle East crude oil tanker rates reached a multi-decade high in March
EIA
In March 2026, tanker rates for Very Large Crude Carriers (VLCCs) leaving the Middle East to Asia were the highest since at least November 2005, when data were first recorded. The price increase followed Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz on March 2.
Market Outlook
The energy complex is trading in a deeply bifurcated fashion today — WTI's 2.51% rally to $96.82 and Brent's 3.36% plunge to $104.38 reflect a market grappling with conflicting signals from Trump's five-day ceasefire pause and Iran's partial reopening of the Strait of Hormuz to 10 tankers. The steep WTI backwardation curve — $96.82 spot versus $86.43 in August — signals traders view the current geopolitical risk premium as temporary and supply normalization as the base case, but that assumption is fragile given the ceasefire deadline has not yet passed. The $25 billion infrastructure repair bill and multi-decade high VLCC tanker rates out of the Middle East underscore that even a diplomatic resolution will not quickly restore full regional supply capacity, leaving a structural upward floor under prices for months. India's dual-policy response — slashing excise duties while levying export taxes — is a leading indicator of demand-side stress in a major Asian importer, and similar moves by other large consumers could complicate the demand outlook if sustained high prices force additional policy interventions. Key macro risks include Trump policy volatility unsettling long-term upstream investment decisions, potential rerouting cost inflation of nearly $5 billion already embedded in global shipping, and the possibility that ceasefire expiration triggers a fresh escalation that sends WTI back toward $110. Watch the Trump ceasefire deadline expiration, any EIA weekly crude inventory print, and further VLCC rate data as the three most actionable near-term catalysts.
