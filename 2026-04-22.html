Top Stories
Oil Whipsaws as Trump Extends Iran Ceasefire, Blockade Holds
CNBC / OilPrice / Upstream Online
Oil prices swung sharply Wednesday after President Trump extended a ceasefire with Iran but maintained the naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, leaving the supply disruption intact. Goldman Sachs warned global crude inventories are drifting toward record lows as tanker traffic through Hormuz remains severely curtailed, with analysts cautioning the inventory draw will continue even if flows partially resume by month-end. Traders are pricing in persistent tightness, with the IEA-coordinated strategic reserve release providing only partial relief.
China's Top Refiners Sell Crude as Refinery Run Rates Crater
OilPrice
Sinopec and Sinochem have begun offloading West African crude grades onto the spot market — a rare move for Chinese majors — as soaring oil prices and constrained Middle East supply force deep cuts to refinery run rates. The selloff targets refiners in Asia and Europe, signaling that Chinese demand destruction is beginning to ripple through global crude flows. The development adds a bearish demand signal to a market already grappling with supply-side shock from the Hormuz closure.
Russia to Cut Kazakh Oil Flows Through Pipeline Supplying Berlin
Financial Times
Moscow is planning to suspend Kazakh crude flows through a key pipeline that supplies the majority of Berlin's petrol, kerosene, and heating fuel, according to the Financial Times. The move threatens to tighten European refined product balances at an already stressed moment for global energy supply, with Hormuz disruptions already straining Atlantic Basin markets. Traders should monitor Druzhba pipeline nominations and German spot fuel markets for immediate price reaction.
Gas Traders Warn Prices Must Rise Further to Balance Market
Upstream Online / NGI
Speaking at the FT Commodities Global Summit, gas traders cautioned that current price levels are insufficient to fully balance the market amid tightening LNG supply and surging demand signals. U.S. natural gas futures held firm Tuesday, supported by subdued Lower 48 production and cooler two-week forecast models, even as Iran peace talks stalled and near-term demand softened. A proposed 17 GW AI data center in West Texas faces an uncertain timeline after a boardroom dispute at Fermi America clouded its contracted 300,000 MMBtu/d Permian gas offtake.
Natural gas inventories at the end of winter heating season were near five-year average
EIA
We estimate the Lower 48 U.S. states began this natural gas injection season (April–October) with 1,890 billion cubic feet (Bcf) of working natural gas in storage, based on interpolated data from our Weekly Natural Gas Storage Report. This level was 3% above the previous five-year (2021–25) average and 3% above last year's end-of-season storage volume.
Market Outlook
The market is now fighting a two-front war: a genuine supply shock from the Hormuz blockade that Goldman Sachs warns is driving global inventories toward record lows, and an accelerating demand destruction signal from China where Sinopec and Sinochem are offloading crude at a pace not seen in years as refinery run rates crater. Brent's 4.47% single-session collapse to $94.08 reflects the market beginning to price in that demand destruction more aggressively, but the IEA strategic reserve release is acknowledged as only partial relief — meaning any escalation in the blockade or tanker incident could reverse today's selloff violently. The Russia-Druzhba pipeline threat adds a second supply vector that specifically pressures European refined product markets; traders should monitor Druzhba nominations closely, as even a partial suspension of Kazakh flows to Berlin would tighten Atlantic Basin diesel and kerosene balances at the worst possible time. For WTI, the inability to obtain US crude inventory data today is a genuine gap — the next EIA report becomes an outsized event, and a surprise build would validate the China demand destruction narrative and likely push WTI through $90 support, while a draw would reassert the Hormuz tightness thesis. Natural gas at $2.72 remains the relative bright spot, with the market structurally supported by the FT Summit consensus that prices must move higher to balance the market, but the Fermi America data center uncertainty removes a significant forward demand anchor and caps near-term upside. Key risk events to track: Druzhba pipeline nomination updates, next EIA crude and gas storage prints, any change in Hormuz naval posture, and Chinese refinery run rate data for July.
