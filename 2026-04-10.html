Top Stories
Hormuz Ceasefire Fails to Reopen Chokepoint, Oil Tops $100
CNBC / Upstream Online
Despite a U.S.-Iran ceasefire announcement, tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains largely halted, keeping physical oil markets under extreme stress. Dated Brent and spot crude benchmarks are signaling acute supply strain that paper markets have not fully captured, with analysts warning the ceasefire alone cannot resolve the logistical and security barriers to resumed flows. President Trump issued a direct warning to Iran to stop charging tolls on tanker transits, underscoring that the diplomatic situation remains volatile.
North Sea Forties Hits Record $147/bbl as Gulf Producers Eye Restart
OilPrice
The physical price of Forties Blend surged to a record $147 per barrel, eclipsing the 2008 high, as the Hormuz supply shock drives buyers to scramble for immediately available Atlantic Basin and North Sea crude. In a tentative sign of eventual normalization, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and Iraq have begun soliciting April and May cargo loading nominations from Asian customers for ports that route through the strait. The moves signal preparation for a resumption of Gulf flows, but physical market stress remains severe in the interim.
Aramco-TotalEnergies 465,000 bpd Refinery Shuttered After Attack
Upstream Online / Financial Times
A major joint refinery operated by Aramco and TotalEnergies with a capacity of 465,000 barrels per day has been shut down following an attack, removing a significant chunk of regional refining output at an already critical moment for global petroleum supply. The shutdown compounds downstream tightness stemming from the Strait of Hormuz disruption, threatening product availability across export markets. The Financial Times separately catalogued roughly 60 energy infrastructure facilities damaged during the broader Middle East conflict.
European Airports Face Jet Fuel Crisis Within Three Weeks
Financial Times
An industry group has warned the European Union that jet fuel reserves at airports across the continent are critically low and could run out within three weeks, a direct consequence of the Hormuz-driven disruption to Middle East crude and refined product flows. The alert raises the prospect of flight cancellations or rationing across European hubs if alternative supply chains cannot be activated quickly. Energy traders should note the signal this sends for aviation fuel crack spreads and distillate demand displacement.
Domestic and international demand drive natural gas production growth
EIA
In our Annual Energy Outlook 2026 (AEO2026), we project U.S. dry natural gas production, which accounted for 38% of total U.S. energy production in 2025, will increase significantly over the next several decades, meeting growing domestic and international natural gas demand.
Market Outlook
Crude markets are caught in a dangerous divergence between subdued futures prices and an explosive physical market reality — Forties at $147/bbl and Brent spot benchmarks signal a supply shock that the $95-$98 paper market has not fully absorbed, creating a high-conviction setup for a sharp upside repricing if Hormuz flows remain blocked beyond this week. The ceasefire has not translated into resumed tanker transits, and President Trump's direct warning to Iran over transit tolls underscores that the diplomatic situation remains a live wire — any escalation or breakdown in talks should be treated as an immediate long catalyst for front-month WTI above $100. The Aramco-TotalEnergies refinery shutdown at 465,000 bpd is the underappreciated risk in today's session: this is not just a crude supply story, it is a products story, and the European jet fuel warning — reserves potentially exhausted within three weeks — points to distillate crack spreads as the highest-conviction trade in the complex right now. On the demand side, Gulf producer loading nominations for April-May cargoes are a tentative green shoot, but physical logistics and security barriers mean any normalization timeline is weeks to months away, not days, limiting the downside relief valve for now. The WTI futures curve's steep contango structure — $97.92 in May collapsing nearly $13 by June and over $17 by July — tells you the market expects resolution but is not certain of it; traders should monitor whether that contango steepens further as a real-time gauge of physical stress. Key risks to the bear case for crude: a verified, enforced reopening of Hormuz shipping lanes or a surprise SPR release coordination among IEA member nations; key upside catalysts: another infrastructure attack, ceasefire collapse, or confirmation that European product rationing has begun.
