Top Stories
Asia Pivots to Russian Oil as US Waiver Eases Supply Crisis
Rigzone / OilPrice
Asian nations are turning to Russian crude for the first time in years after a US waiver permitted purchases on Russian tankers to offset the supply shock from the Middle East conflict. The Philippines imported its first ESPO cargo in nearly six years, South Korea received its first Russian naphtha shipment of the year, and Sri Lanka is in active talks with Moscow. The shift signals a significant realignment in Asian crude sourcing as Hormuz disruptions force buyers to seek alternative barrels.
Oil Seesaws Near $100 as Trump Hints at Iran War Exit
CNBC / World Oil
Crude prices swung violently around $100 per barrel on Wednesday after President Trump signaled openness to exiting the conflict with Iran, even as the Strait of Hormuz remains largely closed to tanker traffic. US gasoline has surged past $4 per gallon for the first time since 2022, representing a more than 30% increase driven by the Iran war. Trump has separately urged allies to join efforts to secure the Strait as tanker attacks continue to threaten global oil flows.
EU Warns Iran War Adding €14bn to Energy Bill, Orders Refineries Open
Financial Times / Rigzone
The European Union has warned that the Iran conflict is adding €14 billion to its energy import bill and cautioned that the bloc must prepare for a prolonged disruption to international energy trade. EU member states have been directed to keep refineries running and accelerate biofuels uptake as an emergency supply measure. Wood Mackenzie separately argued the crisis should prompt the UK government to reconsider its restrictive North Sea policy to bolster domestic energy security.
Asian LNG Imports Post Sharpest Drop Since 2020 Amid Hormuz Chaos
OilPrice
Asian LNG imports fell 8.6% year-on-year in March, the steepest decline since the pandemic-driven collapse of December 2020, as Qatar production outages and Hormuz disruptions simultaneously squeezed supply. Total regional imports came in at 20.6 million tons, with Pakistan registering the largest individual drop due to its heavy dependence on Qatari cargoes. The data underscores the acute vulnerability of spot LNG markets to Middle East supply shocks.
Golden Pass Train 1 Delivers First LNG, Boosting US Export Capacity
World Oil
McDermott and Chiyoda have delivered first LNG from Golden Pass Train 1, marking a significant milestone for US liquefaction export capacity at a time when global LNG supply is under severe strain. The project adds meaningful new volumes to the US export portfolio as Asian and European buyers scramble to secure non-Middle Eastern supply. The timing is particularly consequential given the ongoing Hormuz disruptions curtailing Qatar's export ability.
Market Outlook
The 12.97% single-session collapse in Brent to $103.01 is the dominant story, driven by Trump's Iran war exit signals, but traders should resist reading this as a clean de-escalation trade — the Strait of Hormuz remains largely closed to tanker traffic and physical supply disruption has not materially reversed. WTI's comparatively shallow 1.3% decline to $100.05 has compressed the Brent-WTI spread aggressively, a distortion that warrants close attention as it implies either further WTI downside or a Brent technical bounce from heavily oversold conditions. On the demand side, US gasoline surpassing $4/gallon for the first time since 2022 — a 30%-plus spike — introduces real demand destruction risk that could cap any near-term crude recovery, while the EU's €14 billion energy import cost warning underscores that macroeconomic headwinds from sustained high prices are mounting on both sides of the Atlantic. The Asian crude pivot toward Russian ESPO barrels, including the Philippines' first purchase in nearly six years and South Korea's first Russian naphtha of 2025, is a structural realignment that will dampen Middle Eastern and Atlantic Basin crude demand regardless of how the geopolitical situation resolves. On the supply side, Golden Pass Train 1 delivering first LNG is a tangible positive for non-Qatari supply at a critical moment, though a single train cannot offset the 8.6% YoY drop in Asian LNG imports recorded in March. Key risks to watch: any official confirmation of Iran ceasefire talks or Hormuz reopening would accelerate the selloff toward $90 Brent, while a breakdown in negotiations or fresh tanker attacks could trigger a violent reversal above $110 — position sizing discipline around the $100 WTI and $103 Brent pivots is essential.
