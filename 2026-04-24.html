Brent Surges Past $106 as U.S.-Iran Strait of Hormuz Standoff Deepens
OilPrice / CNBC / World Oil
Brent crude climbed to $106.30/bbl and WTI to $96.68/bbl as the U.S. and Iran contest control of the Strait of Hormuz, disrupting over 10 Bcf/d of energy flows through the critical chokepoint. World Oil
U.S. forces seized an Iranian oil supertanker in the Indian Ocean as Washington intensifies its sanctions enforcement campaign against Tehran's crude exports. The boarding marks an escalation in direct interdiction operations and signals tighter U.S. pressure on Iranian oil flows at sea. Traders should expect further supply disruption risk and elevated geopolitical risk premiums in crude markets.
Golden Pass Ships First LNG Cargo, Boosting U.S. Export Capacity
EIA
Golden Pass LNG, the 10th U.S. liquefaction terminal, shipped its inaugural cargo from Train 1 on April 22, 2026, just 23 days after achieving first LNG production. The facility adds meaningful new export capacity at a critical moment, as Strait of Hormuz disruptions have taken more than 10 Bcf/d of global LNG supply offline. The timing strengthens the U.S. position as a swing LNG supplier to markets scrambling to replace Middle East flows.
Record Storage Build Slams Nymex Gas Futures, Waha Turns Deeply Negative
NGI
A historically large natural gas storage injection snapped a multi-session winning streak for Nymex futures, sending prices sharply lower on Thursday. Waha Hub spot prices collapsed further into deeply negative territory, compounded by pipeline constraints, an Operational Flow Order on Sonat, and persistent Permian Basin oversupply. The triple-digit storage build reinforced bearish fundamentals in the domestic gas market despite the broader geopolitical backdrop supporting LNG export demand.

CNBC

Market Outlook
Crude markets face a bifurcated setup heading into the near term: Brent's violent $6+ reversal from the $106.30 intraday high to a $100.06 close signals that while geopolitical risk premiums are real, the market is not prepared to sustain triple-digit Brent without clearer evidence of sustained, prolonged supply loss. The U.S. seizure of an Iranian supertanker in the Indian Ocean is a meaningful escalation — direct interdiction operations historically ratchet up geopolitical risk premiums by $3-$5/bbl and increase the probability of retaliatory Iranian moves in the Strait, which traders must price as a fat-tail event. WTI's steep contango — front month at $96.71 versus Sep 2026 at $83.43, a $13+ discount — reflects market skepticism that current disruption-driven prices are sustainable, and creates carry incentives for storage plays if onshore U.S. capacity is available. The natural gas market remains fundamentally split: the record storage build and Waha's deeply negative spot prices underscore domestic oversupply, yet Golden Pass LNG's inaugural cargo and the 10+ Bcf/d Hormuz LNG supply hole create a legitimate export-driven bull case for Henry Hub futures if additional trains come online quickly. Key macro risk to watch is India — a failure to finalize a U.S.-India trade deal amid the Section 301 probe threatens one of the fastest-growing LNG and crude demand centers, and could pressure demand-side assumptions embedded in current forward curves. Traders should watch Brent's ability to defend $100, any official statements from Tehran or Washington on Hormuz negotiations, and next week's EIA storage print for confirmation of whether the bearish domestic gas build was a one-week anomaly or the start of a sustained trend.
