Strait of Hormuz Attacks Spark 4% Oil Price Surge Above $90
OilPrice / CNBC / Upstream Online
Multiple cargo ships have been struck near the Strait of Hormuz, with one vessel hit by a projectile and crew evacuating, as shipping traffic through the critical waterway has virtually ground to a halt. Brent crude surged above $90/barrel on Wednesday, rebounding from Tuesday's close of $88, as markets priced in severe supply disruption risk despite reports of an imminent record IEA strategic reserve release. Tankers are also avoiding the UAE port of Fujairah, forcing ADNOC to resell oil cargoes at higher prices as the Gulf war risk reshapes routing and freight markets.
IEA Plans Record Oil Reserve Release as War Triggers Energy Shock
Financial Times / CNBC
The International Energy Agency is preparing its largest-ever coordinated release of strategic oil reserves in response to the energy shock triggered by the U.S.-Iran-Israel conflict, according to the Financial Times. The move underscores deepening concern among policymakers about the impact of Middle East supply disruptions on the global economy. Despite the prospect of the historic reserve release, oil prices continued to climb, with traders focused on the physical supply crunch at the Strait of Hormuz.
U.S.-Iran War Causing Worst Energy Disruption Since 1970s
Rigzone / World Oil / Upstream Online
The escalating conflict between the U.S., Israel, and Iran is creating the most severe disruption to global energy markets since the 1970s oil crisis, according to a GlobalData analysis cited by Rigzone. Oil prices surging above $100/barrel are now raising significant inflation risks for global markets, with central bank forecasters already revising their scenarios for higher energy prices. ExxonMobil has evacuated some workers from the Middle East region, according to the company's CEO.
Natural Gas Spot Prices Hit 16-Month Low on Warm Forecasts
NGI
Spot natural gas prices dropped to a 16-month low on Tuesday as springlike temperatures dominated near-term weather forecasts across most of the U.S., overwhelming any bullish support from the incoming cold shot expected Wednesday. Prompt-month futures also settled lower as weaker European gas prices added pressure, though winter contracts further out the curve found support from ongoing Middle East tensions and potential LNG supply concerns. The divergence between prompt weakness and deferred strength signals that traders are hedging geopolitical supply risk even as near-term fundamentals remain bearish.

Market Outlook
Energy markets are caught in a high-stakes tug-of-war between the most severe Middle East supply disruption since the 1970s and a coordinated policy response that could flood the market with strategic reserves. Brent's 2% decline to $86.06 — despite ships being struck near the Strait of Hormuz and Fujairah port traffic collapsing — signals that the IEA's record reserve release announcement is doing real work in capping the geopolitical risk premium, at least in paper markets. However, physical market stress is acute: ADNOC is being forced to resell cargoes at elevated prices, tanker rerouting is spiking freight costs, and ExxonMobil has begun evacuating personnel from the region — all indicators that the supply crunch is real and deepening. The absence of EIA inventory data is a meaningful gap for directional traders, removing a key weekly anchor at precisely the moment when positioning clarity is most needed. Key risks skew asymmetrically to the upside: any escalation in Hormuz vessel strikes, Iranian retaliation against IEA member infrastructure, or a reserve release that underwhelms market expectations could rapidly reprice Brent back above $90 and toward the $100 level flagged in GlobalData's analysis. Traders should watch the $85.00 level in Brent as the near-term line in the sand — a sustained break below opens the door toward $82, while a failure to hold that level on renewed Hormuz headlines would confirm that physical supply risk has definitively overwhelmed the policy response.
