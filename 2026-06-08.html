Top Stories
Israel Strikes Iranian Petrochemical Plant as Conflict Rapidly Escalates
OilPrice / Rigzone
Israel hit a petrochemical plant in southwestern Iran early Monday — the first direct energy strike on Iranian territory since an April ceasefire — after Iran and Israel exchanged missile barrages over the weekend. The attack triggered an immediate 5% spike in oil prices, with markets focused on escalation risk and potential Strait of Hormuz disruption. Despite Trump's reported calls for restraint, Netanyahu's government pressed forward with retaliatory strikes on multiple Iranian military targets.
Oil Pulls Back as Trump Signals Israel-Iran Ceasefire Possible
CNBC / World Oil / Financial Times
Crude prices retreated from intraday highs Monday after Trump indicated both Israel and Iran are open to a ceasefire, though the conflict is approaching its 100th day with U.S. and Iranian negotiating positions still far apart. Markets remain on edge as Hormuz shipping restrictions continue to limit the physical impact of OPEC+'s fourth consecutive production quota increase. Analysts warn that China's role in capping prices below $100 through reduced imports may not hold if the conflict drags on.
HEP Acquires Full Midship Pipeline Control, Expands Midcontinent Gas Network
NGI
Howard Energy Partners has acquired the remaining stake in the Midship Pipeline, taking full ownership of a key natural gas conduit linking Anadarko Basin production with Gulf Coast and Southeast demand markets. The deal also adds Midcontinent natural gas gathering lines, expanding HEP's footprint in a region where supply growth has been outpacing takeaway capacity. The acquisition positions HEP to capitalize on rising LNG feedgas demand along the Gulf Coast corridor.
China's nuclear power capacity nearly doubled since 2016
EIA
From 2016 to 2024, China's nuclear generation capacity increased 76% (24 GW), based on our International Energy Statistics (IES) data. According to the International Atomic Energy Agency's Power Reactor Information System (PRIS), China added an additional 1.1 GW of nuclear power capacity in 2025 and 2.2 GW in 2026 (through May).

Market Outlook
The market is trading on a razor's edge between a full-scale Middle East supply shock and a diplomatic off-ramp, with WTI at $91.86 and Brent at $94.53 reflecting a measured but meaningful geopolitical risk premium. Israel's strike on an Iranian petrochemical facility — the first energy-sector hit since the April ceasefire — is a qualitative escalation that markets cannot ignore; any follow-on attack targeting Iranian crude export terminals or Kharg Island would almost certainly push Brent through $100 and WTI to the mid-to-upper $90s. The counter-pressure is real: Trump's ceasefire signaling knocked prices off intraday highs, and analysts are flagging China's demand restraint as a structural cap on upside, with Beijing's reduced import appetite — bolstered long-term by a 76% surge in nuclear capacity since 2016 — limiting how far the price can run even in a prolonged conflict. OPEC+'s fourth consecutive quota increase adds to the supply-side buffer, though Hormuz shipping disruptions are already blunting its physical impact, meaning the effective supply cushion is thinner than the headline numbers imply. On the gas side, the HEP-Midship deal is a constructive mid-to-long-term signal for Gulf Coast LNG feedgas supply chains, but it does nothing to support near-term Henry Hub prices, which broke back below $3.20 and are vulnerable to a test of $3.00 if injection-season supply remains ample. Key catalysts to watch: any Israeli or Iranian statement on ceasefire terms, Hormuz transit data, China crude import figures for the month, and the delayed Baker Hughes rig count for directional US supply signals.
