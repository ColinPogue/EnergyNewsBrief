Top Stories
Oil Surges Past $107 as Middle East Infrastructure Attacks Escalate
CNBC / Upstream Online / OilPrice
Crude prices jumped 4% to top $107 per barrel after escalating attacks on Middle East energy infrastructure rattled markets, with Upstream Online separately reporting a 3% rise tied specifically to strikes in the UAE. Iran continues shipping crude through the Strait of Hormuz while simultaneously threatening Gulf neighbors' tankers with destruction if they attempt passage, keeping supply disruption risk elevated. Traders are closely watching whether the conflict broadens, as Japan's PM Takaichi heads into a Thursday summit with Trump where Iran is expected to dominate the agenda.
Ukraine Greenlights Druzhba Oil Flow Resumption in EU Deal
Rigzone
Kyiv has agreed to restore Druzhba pipeline flows to Hungary and Slovakia after EU senior officials linked resumption to the finalization of a €90 billion loan package and a fresh round of Russia sanctions. The agreement signals a rare diplomatic alignment between Ukraine and EU members who had been pushing to restore landlocked energy access, while also tightening the sanctions framework around Moscow. For crude traders, the move could incrementally shift European supply balances and reduce the pressure premium on alternative pipeline routes.
US Waiver Drives India to Snap Up Russian Crude Over China
OilPrice
India is outcompeting China for Russian crude cargoes, with tankers diverting mid-voyage from Chinese destinations toward Indian ports following a U.S. waiver on purchases of Russian oil and a Middle East supply shock pushing Indian refiners back to Moscow's barrels. The Aqua Titan tanker's mid-sea redirect from a Baltic Sea port to India illustrates the dynamic shift in Russian crude flow patterns. This realignment has direct implications for freight markets, Russian crude discounts, and the effectiveness of Western sanctions enforcement.
US Natural Gas Hits Record 92 Bcf/d Consumption in 2025
EIA
U.S. natural gas consumption averaged a record 92.0 Bcf/d across all of 2025, a 2% increase over 2024, while January 2025 alone set a new winter monthly record of 126.6 Bcf/d — up 5% year-on-year — according to EIA's Natural Gas Monthly. The data underscores sustained demand growth driven by power generation, LNG export feedgas, and industrial load even as mild weather forecasts currently have futures traders in a wait-and-see mode. The record consumption baseline adds structural support to longer-term natural gas price outlooks.
INGAA Report Sees Canada’s Natural Gas Exports Surging as Domestic Demand Gets Slashed
NGI
Canada’s natural gas sector is increasingly pivoting away from domestic consumption toward export markets, with pipeline flows to the United States and LNG shipments expected to drive long-term demand growth, according to a report issued by the Interstate Natural Gas Association’s (INGAA) research arm.
Market Outlook
The crude complex is operating in full geopolitical risk-premium mode, with Brent's breach of $108 and 4.47% single-session surge signaling that markets are pricing a non-trivial tail risk of Hormuz disruption — a scenario that would remove roughly 20% of global seaborne crude supply from the market. The unusual Brent-WTI spread widening, with WTI gaining less than 1% against Brent's near 4.5% move, suggests domestic US supply buffers are providing a partial offset, but a sustained Brent rally above $110 will inevitably drag WTI through the $100 handle and reshape the entire forward curve. The Druzhba pipeline resumption story is a mild bearish counterweight for European crude spreads — restoring flow to Hungary and Slovakia incrementally relieves pressure on alternative supply routes and could compress the Urals discount — but this is dwarfed in magnitude by the Middle East risk premium currently being applied. The India-Russia crude diversion dynamic, highlighted by the Aqua Titan mid-voyage redirect, is worth monitoring for its freight market and discount implications: if Indian refiners are consistently outbidding Chinese buyers for Russian barrels on US waiver terms, Urals discounts to Brent could narrow, tightening an arbitrage that has been a structural feature of post-sanctions crude markets. On natural gas, the record 92.0 Bcf/d 2025 consumption baseline from EIA provides durable structural support, but the $3.06 spot price signals traders are not yet willing to price that structural story into the prompt month absent a weather catalyst or LNG export demand spike. Key risk events to watch: Thursday's Trump-Takaichi summit for Iran policy signals, any confirmation of Hormuz transit disruption, weekly EIA storage prints, and Canadian LNG export ramp timelines per the INGAA report which could reshape North American supply-demand balances into 2026.
