Hormuz Crisis: WTI Premiums Soar, Qatar LNG Tankers Turned Back
OilPrice / World Oil / Financial Times
U.S. WTI Midland crude premiums have surged to a record $30–$40/bbl above key regional benchmarks as Asia and Europe scramble for non-Hormuz supply amid the strait's de facto closure. Qatar LNG tankers have been forced to turn back, tightening global gas supply and amplifying the shock to energy markets. Saudi Arabia is simultaneously charging Asian customers record premiums of around $20/bbl above benchmark, reflecting the acute regional supply squeeze.

Israel Strikes Iran's South Pars; UAE Gas Facility Hit in Attack
Upstream Online
Israel has struck a petrochemical plant at Iran's South Pars gas complex, one of the world's largest natural gas fields, marking a significant escalation in direct attacks on Iranian energy infrastructure. Separately, an attack on the UAE's largest gas facility killed one person and caused significant damage, raising fears of broader supply disruptions across Gulf producing nations. Together, the strikes represent a sharp intensification of energy infrastructure warfare in the region.
The Middle East Gulf was source for 8% of 2025 U.S. crude oil imports
EIA
In 2025, the United States imported an average of 490,000 barrels per day (b/d) of crude oil from the Middle East Gulf region—Bahrain, Iraq, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates (UAE). Crude oil imports from the region are primarily medium sour grades of crude oil and flow mainly into the West Coast and Gulf Coast of the United States.

Market Outlook
Energy markets are entering the most consequential week of the current geopolitical cycle, with President Trump's Tuesday ultimatum to Iran serving as the binary catalyst that could either unwind the Hormuz risk premium sharply or trigger a full-blown supply crisis. WTI at $111.80 and Brent at $109.09 are remarkably restrained given the underlying physical market stress — WTI Midland premiums at $30–$40/bbl above benchmark and Saudi OSPs at record $20/bbl above benchmark signal that paper prices have not yet fully absorbed the supply shock hitting Asia and Europe. Israel's strike on South Pars and the attack on the UAE's largest gas facility represent a dangerous escalation in energy infrastructure warfare, threatening to remove meaningful volumes of both crude and LNG from global markets on a sustained basis. The US exposure, while manageable at 490,000 b/d or roughly 8% of 2025 crude imports from the Gulf region — concentrated in medium sour grades flowing to the West and Gulf Coasts — could tighten domestic refinery margins if the disruption persists and alternative medium sour supplies tighten further. Baker Hughes rig count data is unavailable today, removing a key read on whether US producers are accelerating activity in response to the price signal, which itself is a risk to watch. Key catalysts for the week ahead: the Trump-Iran deadline outcome Tuesday, any further strikes on Gulf energy infrastructure, ceasefire proposal developments, and whether physical LNG spot premiums in Asia continue to surge as Qatar tankers remain blocked — a sustained LNG squeeze could finally break Natural Gas out of its $2.83 holding pattern toward $3.00 and beyond.
