Top Stories
Iran Deal Sends Brent Below $80; Hormuz Reopens for Oil Flow
Rigzone / CNBC / World Oil / Upstream Online
Brent crude fell below $80 per barrel — a three-month low — after a US-Iran interim agreement would allow Iran to resume oil exports immediately and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with first laden tankers already departing Iran. The deal, which dominated the G7 summit in France, includes a $300 billion economic development program for Tehran contingent on permanent nuclear negotiations. Oil has now fallen for four straight sessions as traders price in a significant increase in global crude supply.
IEA Warns Peace Deal Could Flip Oil Market Into Glut by 2025
Financial Times / CNBC
The IEA has sharply cut its global oil demand forecast, warning that the Iran War triggered significant demand destruction, and that a gradual resumption of Middle East flows will eventually give way to a sharp rise in production that outstrips demand growth. The agency flagged that once Hormuz flows normalize, a substantial oil surplus could materialize as early as next year. Oil production infrastructure and markets face a months-to-years recovery timeline from the Strait closure, complicating the supply return picture.
Qatar LNG Tankers Return to Ras Laffan as Hormuz Reopening Nears
OilPrice / NGI
At least four Qatari LNG tankers have repositioned toward Ras Laffan in anticipation of the Strait of Hormuz reopening, signaling Qatar's readiness to resume exports as the world's second-largest LNG supplier. However, NGI notes the LNG market outlook remains uncertain, with analysts divided on timing and the pace at which global gas supply chains can normalize following the prolonged closure. Traders should watch feedgas flows and spot LNG pricing closely as the return of Qatari volumes could materially pressure global LNG prices.
US Natural Gas Futures, Cash Prices Rise on Heat and H2 Demand Risks
NGI
July natural gas futures advanced Tuesday as traders began pricing in the risk of simultaneous power sector demand strength and elevated LNG feedgas consumption in the second half of summer. Cash prices at Permian Basin hubs extended a recovery from historically depressed levels, while Southeast and Gulf Coast demand centers posted some of the strongest premiums seen across the Lower 48 this season. The dual demand catalysts — heat load and LNG pull — are underpinning a more constructive near-term price outlook.
Solar generation in CAISO surpassed natural gas in the first five months of 2026
EIA
In the first five months of 2026, utility-scale solar generation surpassed natural gas generation in CAISO. Solar electricity generation in the California Independent System Operator (CAISO) over the first five months of 2026 increased 21% compared with the same period in 2024, and natural gas generation decreased by 60%, data from our Hourly Electric Grid Monitor shows.
Market Outlook
The dominant market narrative has decisively shifted to oversupply risk following the US-Iran interim agreement, with first laden tankers already departing Iranian ports and the Strait of Hormuz functionally reopened — a structural bearish development that four consecutive down sessions in crude have only partially priced in. The IEA's warning of a potential oil glut materializing as early as 2025 adds institutional weight to the bear case, with the agency citing meaningful demand destruction from the Iran War alongside an eventual surge in Middle East production that outpaces demand recovery. Brent's hold above $79 and WTI's $75.60 close are near-term floors to watch closely; failure to hold these levels on increasing Iranian export volumes would likely trigger technical selling toward $76 Brent and $72 WTI. On the supply-return timeline, traders should not underestimate the infrastructure recovery lag the IEA flagged — damaged terminals, disrupted shipping insurance markets, and re-certification of tanker routes could slow the actual barrel ramp even if the diplomatic framework holds, providing intermittent relief rallies. Natural gas presents a divergent setup: domestic fundamentals remain constructive on heat demand and LNG feedgas pull, but the return of Qatari LNG through Hormuz is a medium-term ceiling risk that could materially pressure global spot LNG prices and, by extension, US export netbacks. The structural longer-term bear flag for gas demand is also worth noting — CAISO solar generation surpassing natural gas output in the first five months of 2026 (with gas generation down 60% year-on-year) signals accelerating fuel switching that will structurally pressure power-burn demand in key markets, a trend traders should weight in any H2 2026 strip positioning.
