Top Stories
Trump 'Clock Is Ticking' Warning Sends Oil Prices Surging
CNBC / World Oil
President Trump escalated rhetoric against Iran over the weekend, warning Tehran to 'get moving' or face destruction, reigniting fears of resumed armed conflict and driving oil prices sharply higher. The U.S. and Iran remain deadlocked over a Strait of Hormuz deal, with no resolution in sight as Washington signals potential strikes on civilian infrastructure. Traders are pricing in significant geopolitical risk premium as the impasse threatens to further tighten already strained global crude supplies.
FT: Energy Crunch to Worsen as Iran War Squeezes Supply
Financial Times
The Financial Times warns that the supply squeeze stemming from the Iran war will intensify before conditions improve, with the looming energy crunch posing significant risks to global markets. European oil refiners and airlines are responding by maximizing jet fuel production and ramping up imports from the U.S. and Africa to compensate for lost Middle Eastern supplies, expressing cautious confidence that shortages can be avoided. The dual pressures of constrained Hormuz flows and surging demand are reshaping global crude and products trade flows.
Chinese Refiners Cut Crude Runs to Four-Year Low
OilPrice
China's crude throughput fell 5.8% year-on-year in April to approximately 13.3 million barrels per day, the lowest level since August 2022, as soaring oil prices driven by the Iran war forced refiners to slash utilization and curtail crude imports. The pullback from the world's largest crude importer represents a significant demand-side signal for traders, potentially capping price gains even as geopolitical tensions support the market. Official data from China's National Bureau of Statistics confirmed the steep decline in refinery activity.
Qatar Resumes LNG Exports Through Strait of Hormuz
NGI
Qatar has shipped two LNG cargoes through the Strait of Hormuz over the past week, marking a significant development after supplies were stranded amid the Iran conflict disrupting passage through the critical chokepoint. However, Doha has indicated it has no immediate plans to clear other stranded cargoes, leaving supply uncertainty intact for LNG buyers dependent on Qatari exports. The limited resumption provides only partial relief to a tight global LNG market already grappling with war-driven disruptions.
EIA Widens 2026 Global Oil Deficit Forecast
Rigzone
The U.S. Energy Information Administration has expanded its projected global oil supply deficit for 2026 in its latest Short-Term Energy Outlook, a bullish signal for crude prices amid already elevated geopolitical risk. The wider deficit forecast reflects tightening supply conditions, compounding pressure from Hormuz disruptions and reduced Chinese refinery throughput that are reshaping the global supply-demand balance. For traders, the EIA revision reinforces a structurally undersupplied market outlook heading into next year.
Market Outlook
Markets are sending contradictory signals today, and traders need to parse the Brent-WTI divergence carefully: the $7.86 spread blowout reflects a market simultaneously pricing in geopolitical supply risk via Brent while discounting demand destruction via WTI, and that tension is unlikely to resolve quickly. The China data point is the single most important bearish input this week — 13.3 million bpd in April throughput, down 5.8% year-on-year and the weakest since August 2022, confirms that triple-digit oil prices are forcing tangible demand rationing at the margin, which materially caps the upside even as Hormuz flows remain constrained. On the supply side, Trump's 'clock is ticking' language toward Tehran is not noise — markets are right to price in a meaningful probability of U.S. strikes on Iranian infrastructure, which would further compress Hormuz transit capacity and support Brent toward the $115-$118 range in a shock scenario. The EIA's widened 2026 global supply deficit forecast adds structural ballast to the bull case, reinforcing that the market is heading into next year fundamentally undersupplied even before factoring in active conflict scenarios. The partial Qatari LNG resumption is tactically bullish for natural gas but strategically insufficient — two cargoes against a backdrop of stranded shipments and war-driven disruption means $3.00 nat gas is a floor, not a ceiling, and a sustained move above $3.15 would likely trigger aggressive length-building from institutional desks. Key risks to monitor: any diplomatic breakthrough between Washington and Tehran would rapidly deflate the geopolitical premium and send WTI toward $94-$96; conversely, confirmed U.S. military action against Iranian energy infrastructure is the tail risk that sends Brent through $120 and forces emergency IEA reserve releases.
