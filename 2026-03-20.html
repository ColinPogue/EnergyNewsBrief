Top Stories
IEA Calls Middle East War Greatest Energy Crisis in History
Financial Times / CNBC
IEA chief Fatih Birol has declared the Iran war the gravest threat to global energy supply ever recorded, warning that recovery of oil and gasfields in the Gulf region could take more than six months. The agency is urging consumers to cut fuel use — advising measures such as working from home and driving slower — as it flags 'the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market.' The crisis has sent shockwaves through energy markets globally, with no swift resolution in sight.
Brent Near $109 as Hormuz Closure Enters Third Week
OilPrice
Oil markets are on track for a fifth consecutive weekly gain, with ICE Brent trading around $109 per barrel as the Strait of Hormuz remains shut for a third week, keeping global supply critically tight. Roughly one-fifth of the world's oil flows through the chokepoint, and analysts warn that no combination of alternative routes, strategic reserves, or spare capacity can fully replace Hormuz volumes over an extended closure. Policy efforts to ease markets have so far failed to arrest the rally.
Ras Laffan Attacks Threaten Global LNG Supply for Up to Five Years
World Oil / Upstream Online
Missile strikes on Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG complex have triggered a supply crisis that could reshape global LNG markets, with QatarEnergy warning repairs to damaged plants may take up to five years to complete. The extended outage timeline is already reverberating through LNG spot and forward markets worldwide, threatening long-term supply contracts and forcing buyers to scramble for alternatives. The disruption compounds the existing Middle East energy crisis and adds severe upward pressure on global gas prices.
U.S. Natural Gas Prices Slide as Supply Buffers Middle East Shock
NGI
Despite war-driven spikes in global gas prices, U.S. Henry Hub forward prices have declined as record domestic production, healthy storage inventories, and the onset of the spring shoulder season outweigh geopolitical risk. An outsized South Central storage injection significantly narrowed the supply deficit versus historical norms, adding further bearish pressure to Permian Basin cash prices. Traders are watching whether a prolonged Middle East conflict and LNG export disruptions could eventually tighten the domestic supply-demand balance enough to reverse the downtrend.
Wind and solar generated a record 17% of U.S. electricity in 2025
EIA
Over the past 20 years, electricity from wind power and utility-scale solar power has increased to 17% of generation in the United States compared to less than 1% in 2005. In 2025, net generation of wind and solar together accounted for 760,000 gigawatthours (GWh) of electricity, 88,000 GWh more than in 2024, according to data from our Electric Power Monthly.
Market Outlook
Energy markets are sending deeply conflicting signals today: Brent's $3.67 collapse to $104.98 despite a third consecutive week of Hormuz closure and the IEA declaring this the worst supply disruption in recorded history suggests that demand destruction fears and forced liquidation are now competing directly with the geopolitical risk premium. The Brent-WTI spread blowing out to approximately $8.45 is a critical tell — U.S. domestic supply buffers and record production are shielding Henry Hub and WTI from the full force of the Gulf crisis, while internationally exposed benchmarks bear the brunt of both supply shock and demand anxiety. The WTI futures curve's steep backwardation — $97.50 in Apr 2026 collapsing to $85.89 by Aug 2026 — confirms the market expects near-term tightness to eventually resolve, but the IEA's warning that Gulf field recovery could take more than six months keeps tail risk firmly to the upside on Brent. On natural gas, the domestic picture remains bearish in isolation, with the South Central storage injection materially narrowing the supply deficit and spring shoulder demand weighing on Henry Hub near $3.08; however, the Ras Laffan five-year repair timeline is a slow-burning upside catalyst that could eventually tighten U.S. LNG export feed gas demand and reverse the current downtrend. Key risks to monitor: any escalation widening the Hormuz closure timeline beyond six months would invalidate the current backwardation pricing and send Brent toward $120+; conversely, a diplomatic resolution or coordinated SPR release could collapse the war premium and drive Brent sharply toward $95. Tomorrow's session watch: U.S. rig count data, any IEA member SPR release announcements, and Hormuz transit reports will be the primary price catalysts.
