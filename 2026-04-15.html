Top Stories
U.S. Hormuz Blockade 'Fully Implemented'; Iran Output at Risk Within Weeks
CNBC / Financial Times / OilPrice
The White House declared the Strait of Hormuz blockade fully implemented while simultaneously signaling a diplomatic off-ramp for Iran, as negotiations with Tehran remain active. The Financial Times reports Iran has a maximum of 16 days of crude storage before it must curtail production, putting roughly 1.5–1.7 mb/d of Iranian exports at risk. A supertanker, the Agios Fanourios I, made a second attempt Wednesday to transit into the Persian Gulf to load Iraqi crude, potentially becoming the first vessel to breach the blockade.
FT: Prolonged Hormuz Closure Threatens Global Oil Supply Chains
Financial Times
A Financial Times analysis warns that a prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz would create cascading oil shortages beyond the immediate region, with later-affected markets potentially suffering the most severe disruptions. The piece highlights that alternative routing options are limited and that the full demand destruction impact would be felt unevenly across importing nations over time. Traders are advised that price volatility could intensify well beyond the initial supply shock.
Indonesia Pivots to Russian Crude as Middle East Supply Risk Grows
OilPrice
Indonesia confirmed it will increase purchases of Russian crude and LPG following a presidential-level meeting between the two nations, directly citing elevated Middle East supply risks as the driver. Energy Minister discussions flagged long-term supply agreements as the framework being negotiated, deepening Russia's foothold in Southeast Asian oil markets. The move reflects a broader realignment of crude flows as Hormuz disruptions pressure traditional supply routes.
BP CEO Overhauls Structure, Returns Focus to Core Upstream-Downstream
World Oil
BP CEO Meg O'Neill announced a significant organizational restructuring aimed at simplifying the company and refocusing its business model on traditional upstream exploration and downstream refining operations. The move marks a strategic retreat from BP's broader energy transition positioning under previous leadership and comes amid an industry-wide reassessment of capital allocation given elevated oil prices. Market participants will watch for implications on BP's asset portfolio, project pipeline, and future production guidance.
Hydropower generation expected to recover despite snow drought in the West
EIA
In our April Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO), we expect U.S. hydropower generation will increase by 5% in 2026 but remain 1.8% below the 10-year average following snow drought conditions in some states. Hydropower generation in 2025 increased to 245 billion kilowatthours (BkWh), about 4 BkWh more than the record-low generation year 2024.
Market Outlook
The Hormuz blockade narrative is now the single dominant price driver across crude markets, and the 16-day Iranian storage ceiling identified by the FT creates a hard, near-term catalyst timeline — traders should expect volatility to compress and then violently reprice as that window closes or diplomatic negotiations produce a breakthrough. With 1.5–1.7 mb/d of Iranian exports at risk, even a partial sustained disruption would represent a material supply shock against an already tight global balance, and Brent's approach toward the $97–$98 resistance band reflects the market beginning to price that tail risk. Indonesia's pivot to Russian crude is a signal that secondary supply realignment is already underway, which could structurally redirect Urals flows and reduce the effective discount that Russian barrels have traded at, adding further upside pressure to global benchmarks. The BP restructuring under CEO Meg O'Neill is a medium-term bullish signal for upstream supply growth — a return to core E&P focus from a major suggests industry confidence in sustained elevated prices — but this is a 12–18 month story and will not move near-term price action. Key risks skew asymmetric to the upside: a failed diplomatic off-ramp with Iran, an incident involving the Agios Fanourios I supertanker breaching or being stopped at the blockade, or a broader escalation involving other Gulf producers could send Brent through $100 rapidly. Traders should watch the Iran negotiation timeline closely, monitor any vessel transit developments in the Persian Gulf, and flag the missing US crude inventory data as a gap that could produce a sharp knee-jerk reaction on release.
