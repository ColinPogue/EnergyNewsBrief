Top Stories
Iran Threatens US Forces at Hormuz as Oil Prices Surge
OilPrice / Upstream Online / Financial Times
Iran's Armed Forces warned Monday they would attack U.S. vessels approaching or entering the Strait of Hormuz after President Trump launched 'Project Freedom' to escort stranded ships out of the Gulf. Oil prices climbed on the escalation, while ExxonMobil's CEO cautioned that markets have not yet felt the full impact of the Middle East war. The conflict has already disrupted Gulf energy logistics, with UAE fertiliser giant Fertiglobe rerouting product by land truck to bypass the blocked strait.
OPEC+ Approves Limited Output Hike After UAE Departure
World Oil
OPEC+ has approved a limited production increase following the UAE's exit from the group, a significant structural shift for the alliance that has managed global supply through a period of extreme market volatility. The decision signals internal fractures within the cartel at a time when Hormuz disruptions are already tightening global crude supply. Traders will be watching closely for how much additional barrels actually reach market given ongoing geopolitical constraints.
DOE SPR Releases Hit Highest Rate Since October 2022
EIA
The U.S. Department of Energy released 17.5 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve between late March and late April, with 7.1 million barrels drawn in the week ending April 24 alone — the largest single-week release since October 2022. The accelerated drawdown reflects Washington's effort to counter supply tightness stemming from the Hormuz crisis and elevated crude prices. Traders should monitor SPR stock levels closely as the buffer against supply shocks continues to erode.
Golden Pass LNG Train 2 on Track for Year-End Completion
NGI
ExxonMobil expects Golden Pass LNG Train 2 to reach mechanical completion by year-end, with a third train to follow within the next year, adding significant U.S. export capacity as global LNG markets tighten amid the Hormuz disruption. The Texas facility's progress is critical for buyers in Europe and Asia seeking to replace displaced Gulf supplies. Physical natural gas cash prices pulled back Monday, however, as traders weighted milder near-term weather against longer-term supply tightness.
India Burns More Coal as Iran War Squeezes LNG Supply
CNBC
India is ramping up coal-fired power generation as extreme heat drives electricity demand higher and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz restricts LNG supply access, CNBC reports. The world's third-largest CO2 emitter is leaning harder on domestic coal reserves as imported energy costs surge, reducing its near-term LNG buying capacity and reshaping regional gas trade flows. The development adds further complexity to global energy balances already strained by the Middle East conflict.
Market Outlook
The crude complex is repricing Hormuz closure risk in real time, with Brent at $112.09 and WTI at $105.28 reflecting a market that is no longer treating the strait disruption as a tail risk but as a baseline scenario. Iran's explicit threat to attack U.S. naval vessels operating under 'Project Freedom' materially raises the probability of a sustained supply shock — the strait handles roughly 20% of global oil trade, and any kinetic escalation would send both benchmarks sharply higher, with $120 Brent a credible near-term target. OPEC+'s approval of a limited output hike is structurally complicated by the UAE's departure from the group and by the reality that additional barrels mean little if Hormuz remains effectively closed to transit; traders should not bank on OPEC+ volumes as a meaningful offset to supply tightness. The DOE's SPR drawdown — 17.5 million barrels released between late March and late April — is eroding Washington's shock-absorption capacity at an accelerating rate, and with Baker Hughes rig count data unavailable today, domestic production trajectory remains a key unknown. On the demand side, India's coal pivot reduces near-term LNG offtake but does not dampen crude demand meaningfully, while extreme heat across South Asia continues to support overall energy consumption. Key catalysts to watch this week: any U.S. military response to Iran's Hormuz threats, weekly EIA crude inventory data for confirmation of tightening balances, and SPR stock levels to gauge how much buffer Washington retains before policy options narrow further.
