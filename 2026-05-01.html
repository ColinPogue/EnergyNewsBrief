U.S. Firms Sign Deals to Operate in Venezuela's Orinoco
OilPrice
Hunt Overseas Oil Company and Crossover Energy have signed operational agreements with Venezuela's government, marking a notable re-entry of U.S. firms into the country holding the world's largest crude reserves. The Orinoco Belt's heavy crude potential remains a significant long-term supply variable for global markets.
SPR Releases Hit Highest Weekly Rate Since October 2022
EIA
The U.S. DOE released 7.1 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve in the week ending April 24, the largest single-week drawdown since October 2022, bringing total releases since March 20 to 17.5 million barrels. The accelerated pace signals Washington's intent to counter supply tightness driven by the Iran conflict and associated shipping disruptions. Traders should monitor SPR stock levels closely as the release program's ceiling approaches.

Market Outlook
The Iran ceasefire confirmation is the dominant macro event, but traders should resist pricing in a clean resolution — the Strait of Hormuz disruption remains active, and the ceasefire's 60-day War Powers clock pause means the geopolitical premium has been deferred, not extinguished. Brent's $15 round-trip from Thursday's $126/bbl spike to today's $111.16 close reflects exactly this ambiguity, and the spread between Brent and WTI compressing sharply on the day suggests U.S.-specific supply dynamics, including the DOE's 7.1 million barrel SPR release — the heaviest weekly pace since October 2022 — are providing a domestic cushion that Brent lacks. Cumulative SPR releases of 17.5 million barrels since March 20 are meaningful, but traders must track remaining reserve headroom carefully; the program cannot run indefinitely, and any signal of a drawdown ceiling will remove a key bearish offset to Hormuz-driven supply risk. On the supply side, the U.S. re-entry into Venezuela's Orinoco Belt via Hunt Overseas and Crossover Energy deals is a long-duration variable — Orinoco heavy crude production ramp-up timelines are measured in years, not quarters, so this is not a near-term bearish catalyst but it shifts the medium-term supply ceiling higher and is worth flagging in structural models. Natural gas remains a secondary focus but warrants attention: the largest single-session gain since March 11 on maintenance outages and supportive storage data suggests the $2.77 floor is being tested from the upside, and any weather-driven demand catalyst or further pipeline restrictions could accelerate a move toward $3.00. Key risks to watch: ceasefire collapse or Hormuz escalation (sharply bullish Brent, $120+ re-test), SPR release program hitting capacity (removes bearish buffer for WTI), and macro demand destruction signals from China or Europe that could undercut the physical bid supporting current price levels.
