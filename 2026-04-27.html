Top Stories
Iran Talk Collapse Sends Brent Past $108, Hormuz Near Zero
CNBC / World Oil / Rigzone
President Trump cancelled U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner's planned Islamabad trip for Iran negotiations Saturday, causing Brent crude to briefly spike above $108/barrel. Tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains near zero amid competing U.S.-Iran blockades during a fragile ceasefire, with Baker Hughes warning the strait may not fully reopen until the second half of 2026. SEB's chief commodities analyst warns alarm bells will ring loudly if Hormuz does not reopen during May, as the disruption has already affected over 10 Bcf/d of energy flows.
Hormuz Oil Shock Mounts as Billion-Barrel Supply Loss Looms
World Oil
The prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz is threatening both supply and demand simultaneously, with cumulative oil flow disruptions now approaching billion-barrel scale, according to World Oil. The shock is amplifying pressure on global crude benchmarks while raising fears of demand destruction if high prices persist through Q2 and into summer. Traders are watching whether diplomatic progress or further escalation shapes the price trajectory in the near term.
US Sanctions Hit Hengli Petrochemical Over Iranian Crude Purchases
OilPrice
The United States imposed sanctions on Hengli Petrochemical, one of China's largest independent refiners, alleging the company purchased Iranian crude oil in violation of U.S. sanctions, sending its shares down 10% in a single session. Hengli denied the allegations, stating it has never engaged in trade with Iran and that its suppliers provide compliance guarantees. The move signals intensifying U.S. enforcement pressure on Chinese teapot and independent refiners amid the broader Iran crisis.
Golden Pass LNG Ships First Cargo as Hormuz Disruption Bites
EIA
Golden Pass LNG, the 10th U.S. LNG export terminal, shipped its inaugural cargo from Train 1 on April 22, 2026, just 23 days after achieving first production in March. The milestone adds new U.S. export capacity to global markets at a critical moment, as Hormuz disruptions have taken more than 10 Bcf/d of LNG flow offline. The timing positions U.S. Gulf Coast LNG as a key swing supplier for buyers scrambling to replace Middle East volumes.
Permian Gas Crashes to 17-Month Low on Force Majeure Outage
NGI
Permian Basin natural gas spot prices collapsed Friday after a fresh force majeure restricted northbound pipeline capacity, pushing regional prices back toward record lows and dragging the national average to its lowest level in 17 months. The sell-off contrasted with firmer Gulf Coast basis pricing at the open of May bidweek trading, where LNG-driven demand continued to support prices despite loose shoulder-season fundamentals. Traders will be watching whether the Permian pipeline constraint is resolved quickly or extends into bidweek and pressures May index prices.
Market Outlook
The energy complex is navigating a historically rare simultaneous supply-and-demand shock, with Hormuz closure now threatening billion-barrel cumulative oil flow losses and over 10 Bcf/d of LNG effectively offline. Brent's sharp $5 reversal from the $108 spike is technically bearish in the near term, but the $100 level represents a critical battleground — a sustained break below it would signal that demand destruction fears are winning the narrative, while any credible diplomatic signal on Hormuz could rapidly reprice the risk premium back toward $105-108. WTI's relative outperformance and the widening $5-plus Brent-WTI spread is a structural trade to watch; US Gulf Coast barrels are increasingly the marginal supplier of choice as Middle East flows remain constrained and US sanctions enforcement on Chinese teapot refiners like Hengli tightens the Iranian crude arbitrage. Golden Pass LNG's first cargo is a genuine inflection point for US LNG export capacity and a bullish medium-term structural catalyst for Henry Hub, though the immediate nat gas rally faces a ceiling from loose shoulder-season fundamentals and Permian oversupply until the pipeline force majeure is resolved. The top macro risk remains escalation in the Strait — Baker Hughes has warned Hormuz may not fully reopen until H2 2026, and SEB's alarm bell warning for May means any negative headline out of Tehran or Washington in the next two to three weeks could be a violent re-pricing event. Traders should monitor the May bidweek index settlement, any update on Permian pipeline restoration, and whether the US-Iran ceasefire holds past the weekend before adding directional length at current levels.
