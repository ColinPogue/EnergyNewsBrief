Top Stories
Iran-Hormuz Deal 'Largely Negotiated,' Oil Slides 7%
CNBC / OilPrice / Financial Times / World Oil / Upstream Online
President Trump declared a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz 'largely negotiated,' triggering a 7% collapse in crude oil prices and a 5% drop in European natural gas futures to around €46.3/MWh, their lowest in two weeks. A fragile ceasefire has held since April 8, but market veterans warn that even with an agreement, supply normalization will take time — with Asia already near tank-bottom inventory levels and the U.S. potentially facing shortages by July. Carlyle's Jeff Currie cautioned that the structural tightness in physical markets has not been resolved by diplomatic optimism alone.
Iran War Squeezes Sulphur, Fertiliser Groups Cut Output
Financial Times
Major fertiliser producers are curtailing production as the Iran conflict disrupts sulphur supply chains, a key feedstock for phosphate fertilisers. The squeeze threatens crop yields in lower-income countries that depend on affordable phosphate, adding a downstream commodity dimension to the Hormuz crisis beyond crude oil. The development signals widening collateral damage from the conflict across global commodity markets.
Natural Gas Futures Weaken on Supply Overhang, Holiday Demand Lull
NGI
U.S. natural gas futures ended the week on a bearish note as traders digested robust supply data and soft demand expectations heading into the Memorial Day holiday weekend, with spot prices for Saturday-through-Tuesday delivery falling on forecasts of mild temperatures. June bidweek trading opened mixed, with Waha basis discounts easing from May lows while Midwest and Gulf Coast prices weakened and Southeast premiums firmed ahead of anticipated cooling demand. The supply overhang remains the dominant near-term headwind for the prompt contract.

Market Outlook
The Iran-Hormuz deal headline is the single most important near-term variable in crude pricing, and today's 7% intraday crash followed by Brent closing at exactly $100.21 tells you the market is deeply uncertain about how to price deal probability versus execution risk. Carlyle's Jeff Currie is right to flag that diplomatic optimism does not equal supply normalization — Asia is reportedly near tank-bottom inventory levels, and the U.S. faces a potential shortage window by July if Iranian barrels don't flow quickly and smoothly. That physical tightness creates a floor under prices even in a deal scenario, meaning the $95-$97 zone for WTI and the $98-$100 zone for Brent represent high-conviction buy zones for traders willing to fade the diplomatic headline risk. On the supply side, Baker Hughes' 21-rig week-on-week jump in North America adds a medium-term bearish overhang — operators appear to be locking in activity now, and if that drilling momentum translates into incremental U.S. production over the next 60-90 days, it compounds downward price pressure should Iran barrels also return. Natural gas at $3.02 is a separate story driven by domestic fundamentals: the supply overhang and holiday demand lull are structural near-term headwinds, but any sustained heat event post-Memorial Day that drives cooling demand could quickly tighten the prompt contract and push prices back toward $3.25-$3.30. Key risks to watch: deal collapse or Hormuz re-escalation (immediate $5-$8 crude spike), confirmation of Iranian export resumption timeline, July U.S. inventory data, and whether the North America rig count build accelerates into a production growth signal over the next two to three weekly reports.
