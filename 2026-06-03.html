Top Stories
Iranian Strike on Kuwait Airport Sends Oil Higher Third Straight Session
OilPrice / Rigzone / Upstream Online
Iranian drones and missiles struck Kuwait International Airport overnight, killing at least one person, causing significant damage, and prompting suspension of air traffic — marking one of the most direct Iranian attacks on a Gulf neighbor in the current conflict. Oil extended gains for a third consecutive session as market pessimism deepened over prospects for a U.S.-Iran deal that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz, according to Saxo Bank's Ole Hansen. Traders are watching the Strait of Hormuz situation closely as geopolitical risk premiums remain elevated despite prices holding well below $100 per barrel.
Why Oil Stays Below $100 Despite Hormuz Tensions
CNBC
Despite ongoing Iran-related disruption risks and the Strait of Hormuz crisis, three key factors are keeping crude prices from breaching $100 per barrel, according to CNBC's analysis. Demand-side headwinds, OPEC supply dynamics, and macro uncertainty are each playing a role in capping the rally. Traders are weighing these bearish offsets against escalating geopolitical flashpoints in the Gulf.
Delfin LNG Nears FID as Samsung Heavy Lands $2.9B FLNG Contract
NGI
Samsung Heavy Industries disclosed a $2.9 billion contract to deliver a floating LNG export vessel to a North American company, widely seen as pointing to an imminent final investment decision for Delfin LNG off the U.S. Gulf Coast. The Delfin project would be among the first offshore FLNG export facilities in the United States, adding to the growing slate of U.S. LNG export capacity under development. An FID would represent a significant milestone for U.S. natural gas export infrastructure and long-term demand for Gulf Coast gas supply.
LNG Canada Phase 2 Gets Green Light; INEOS Seals Asia Supply Deal
World Oil / Rigzone
Fluor's joint venture has received a formal notice to proceed on LNG Canada Phase 2, advancing one of the largest LNG expansion projects in North America toward construction. Separately, INEOS signed a deal with Japanese conglomerate Marubeni to supply LNG to the Asia-Pacific region, marking the company's first deliveries to that market. Together, the developments underscore accelerating momentum in LNG export infrastructure and trans-Pacific supply agreements.
California natural gas prices reach historic lows in early 2026
EIA
Monthly average natural gas spot prices in California reached record lows in the first five months of 2026, dropping to values last recorded in the nationwide low-price market of 2024. Record lows were recorded in Northern California's PG&E Citygate and Southern California's SoCal Border Average; SoCal Citygate prices fell to near-record lows but remained higher than some 2024 prices.
Market Outlook
Crude markets are running on a geopolitical premium that is simultaneously powerful and fragile — WTI at $95.76 and Brent at $98.00 reflect three sessions of Iran-driven fear, but the failure to crack $100 on Brent is itself a signal worth respecting. The Iranian strike on Kuwait International Airport is the most direct Iranian attack on a Gulf neighbor in the current conflict cycle, and Ole Hansen at Saxo Bank is right to flag that deal pessimism on Hormuz is doing real work in the bid; any further kinetic escalation targeting shipping lanes or Gulf energy infrastructure could be the catalyst that finally breaks $100 on Brent. However, CNBC's three-factor framework — demand headwinds, OPEC supply dynamics, and macro uncertainty — remains structurally intact, and traders should not mistake momentum for a trend change without confirmation above $100 Brent or $97 WTI on a closing basis. The absence of US crude inventory data today is a notable gap; a large surprise build when the data does print could be the pin that deflates the geopolitical risk premium quickly, particularly if Hormuz fears recede even modestly. On natural gas, the California historic low-price prints are a bearish anchor on the spot market, but the Delfin LNG $2.9B Samsung Heavy contract and LNG Canada Phase 2 notice to proceed are structural long-term demand signals that should keep a floor under the back of the curve and support Henry Hub above $3.00 near term. Key watches: any Strait of Hormuz shipping disruption confirmation, the next US EIA crude inventory print, and whether Brent can sustain a close above $99 — that would shift the technical and psychological narrative materially.
