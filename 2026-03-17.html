Top Stories
Iran Drone Strike Halts UAE Shah Gas Field, Tanker Hit Near Hormuz
OilPrice / CNBC / Financial Times / Rigzone / Upstream Online
Iran escalated its drone-strike campaign against Gulf energy infrastructure, setting ablaze the UAE's Shah gas field in Abu Dhabi and striking a tanker near the Strait of Hormuz — marking the first direct attack on a producing field in the UAE. Oil prices advanced on the news as market participants assessed widening supply disruption risk, with Standard Chartered raising its Brent forecast and warning there are 'no clear off-ramps' in the conflict. The cumulative financial cost to Gulf energy producers has exceeded $15 billion, according to analytics firms.
Diesel Hits $5/Gallon; Hormuz Coalition Plan Faces Credibility Test
CNBC / OilPrice
U.S. diesel prices surged to $5 per gallon — the highest since 2022 — as Hormuz supply disruptions ripple through refined product markets, with fuel prices expected to stay elevated until crude flows normalize. Oil pared earlier gains Tuesday as doubts mounted over the effectiveness of a U.S.-backed coalition to protect Strait of Hormuz shipping lanes, with traders skeptical the plan can be executed quickly enough to restore market confidence. Analysts warn crude could push toward $200 per barrel if the disruption proves prolonged, a scenario Tehran's military posture appears designed to keep on the table.
Ukraine Allows Druzhba Pipeline Repairs Under EU Pressure
Financial Times / Upstream Online
Ukraine has agreed to permit repair work on the Druzhba pipeline — which historically carried Russian crude to Hungary and Slovakia — following sustained European Union pressure on Kyiv to restore the route. The reversal marks a significant policy shift with direct implications for Central European refinery supply chains that have struggled to source alternative crudes since the war began. Traders should monitor whether Hungary and Slovakia's longstanding calls for resumed flows translate into actual crude movements in coming weeks.
North America Needs 40,000 Miles of New Gas Pipeline by 2052, INGAA Says
NGI
A new INGAA study projects North America must build nearly 40,000 miles of new natural gas pipeline and expand transmission capacity by 39% to meet surging LNG export and data center demand through 2052, requiring over $1 trillion in total midstream investment. The findings underscore the scale of infrastructure buildout needed to support the U.S. LNG export boom, which received a further boost as the DOE authorized Venture Global's Plaquemines terminal to export higher LNG volumes to NFTA-country buyers. For gas traders, the long-term supply picture is constructive, though near-term Western Canada spot prices weakened Monday as seasonal U.S. import demand declined.
EIA refines estimates for Permian tight oil and shale gas production
EIA
We added the Avalon, Barnett, Dean, and Woodford plays within the Permian Basin to our estimates by formation for Permian tight oil and shale natural gas production in our March 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO). Market Outlook
The dominant market theme heading into the back half of the week is unambiguous: Hormuz risk is repricing global crude, and the Brent-WTI spread blowout to roughly $7.60 tells you exactly where the market is feeling it. Iran's strike on the UAE Shah gas field is a qualitative escalation — attacking a producing field on sovereign UAE territory is a different order of magnitude than harassing tankers — and Standard Chartered is already revising Brent forecasts higher with analysts openly modeling a path toward $200 if disruptions prove prolonged. U.S. diesel at $5 per gallon, its highest since 2022, confirms the disruption is already transmitting into refined product markets, and prices are unlikely to normalize until crude flows through Hormuz are credibly secured — a timeline the U.S.-backed coalition plan, which traders are greeting with open skepticism, has yet to establish. On the supply side, the Druzhba pipeline repair agreement is a modest constructive signal for Central European refinery margins, but actual crude flows through that route remain weeks away at best and will not move the global balance needle while Hormuz dominates the tape. The EIA's expanded Permian formation estimates — now incorporating Avalon, Barnett, Dean, and Woodford plays alongside Spraberry, Bone Spring, and Wolfcamp — could incrementally revise U.S. tight oil production estimates higher in coming STEOs, which is the primary structural cap on WTI upside and explains its underperformance versus Brent today. Key risk events to watch: any further Iranian strikes or retaliatory action by Gulf states, weekly EIA inventory data for confirmation of tightening balances, and credibility signals — or lack thereof — from the Hormuz coalition rollout.
