Top Stories
Oil Surges Above $96 as U.S.-Iran Clash Shuts Hormuz
OilPrice / CNBC / World Oil / Upstream Online
Brent crude jumped more than 6% to over $96/bbl after U.S. and Iranian forces attacked commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, effectively closing the critical waterway to shipping traffic. Kuwait declared force majeure on oil export flows as the disruption rippled through Gulf supply chains, raising fears of a sustained global energy shock. The IEA coordinated an emergency strategic reserve release in response, with the U.S. and member nations tapping stockpiles to offset lost barrels.
EIA: U.S. LNG Exports to Grow 30% by 2027 as Projects Ramp
EIA
The EIA's latest Short-Term Energy Outlook projects U.S. LNG export capacity to grow nearly 30% by the end of 2027 as five new liquefaction projects come online, with net natural gas exports rising 18% to 18.7 Bcf/d. Pipeline exports to Mexico are also forecast to increase, adding another demand channel for U.S. gas producers. The expansion reinforces the U.S. position as the world's dominant LNG supplier at a moment when Hormuz disruptions are reshuffling global energy flows.
ERCOT Power Demand to Triple by 2029 on Data Center Gas Surge
NGI
ERCOT is forecasting peak power demand to hit 278,003 MW by 2029, more than triple the all-time record of 85,508 MW set in August 2023, driven largely by explosive growth in data center load across Texas. The surge points to structurally higher natural gas demand in the state's power sector for years ahead, a critical bullish signal for gas traders focused on domestic consumption. Analysts note that new gas-fired generation capacity will need to come online at scale to meet the projected load.
UK Moves to Delink Gas and Power Prices Amid Iran War Spike
Financial Times
UK Energy Secretary Ed Miliband unveiled plans to sever the link between natural gas prices and retail electricity bills, a structural reform now carrying added urgency as the U.S.-Iran conflict pushes European gas benchmarks sharply higher. The move is aimed at protecting Labour's pledge to reduce household heating costs, which is under threat as Hormuz-driven supply fears feed through to TTF and NBP markets. The policy shift could have lasting implications for how gas price volatility is transmitted to European end consumers.
USA Rare Earth to buy Brazil's Serra Verde for $2.8 billion to build supply outside Asia
CNBC
USA Rare Earth on Monday announced plans to buy Brazilian rare earths miner Serra Verde in a deal worth $2.8 billion in cash and shares.
Market Outlook
The Hormuz closure is the dominant market narrative and the most significant supply-side shock the oil complex has faced in years — with roughly 20% of global seaborne crude transiting the strait, even a partial or temporary closure justifies a sustained geopolitical risk premium well above prior trading ranges. Brent's intraday breach of $96 and the IEA's emergency reserve coordination signal that both markets and policymakers are treating this as a structural disruption, not a one-day headline spike. WTI's underperformance relative to Brent — with the spread widening sharply — reflects the partial insulation of U.S. domestic supply from the Hormuz bottleneck, but any escalation that threatens broader Gulf export infrastructure or Red Sea routing alternatives would close that gap quickly and push WTI firmly through $90. On natural gas, the story is increasingly one of converging bullish catalysts: the Hormuz-driven European gas spike is lifting global LNG benchmarks at exactly the moment U.S. export capacity is set to expand 30% by 2027, while the ERCOT demand explosion — forecast peak load of 278,003 MW by 2029 versus an 85,508 MW all-time record — sets the stage for structurally higher domestic gas demand. Key risks to the upside include Hormuz remaining closed beyond 72 hours, additional Gulf producers declaring force majeure, or Iranian retaliation targeting Saudi or UAE export infrastructure; key downside risks center on a rapid diplomatic off-ramp, faster-than-expected IEA reserve deployment, or demand destruction signals from major consuming nations. Watch the $90 WTI level, the $100 Brent threshold, and the $2.85 natural gas resistance band as the next major technical and psychological triggers that will define whether this is a spike or a regime change.
