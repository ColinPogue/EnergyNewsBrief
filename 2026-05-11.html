Brent Tops $103 as Trump Rejects Iran Peace Response
CNBC / Rigzone / World Oil / Upstream Online
Brent crude surged past $103/bbl Monday after President Trump dismissed Iran's reply to a U.S. peace proposal, with Israel signaling the conflict remains active. Traders are rapidly adding back a supply risk premium, while GPS signal spoofing and erratic ship tracking data in the Persian Gulf point to escalating Hormuz tensions. The combination of renewed geopolitical uncertainty and physical disruption risks is driving fresh upward pressure on crude benchmarks.
Morgan Stanley Warns Oil Market Buffers Nearing Exhaustion
OilPrice
Morgan Stanley says the key market buffers — reduced Chinese crude imports and surging U.S. exports — that have so far prevented oil futures from hitting record highs could be exhausted before the Strait of Hormuz reopens, putting the market in a 'race against time.' If those offsets fade while Hormuz remains closed, the bank warns prices could spike sharply beyond current levels. Traders should monitor U.S. export volumes and Chinese import data closely as leading indicators of buffer capacity.
Europe's Oil Majors Bank Up to $4.75B Trading Iran War Volatility
Financial Times
BP, Shell, and TotalEnergies have collectively reaped up to $4.75 billion from their trading desks capitalizing on price swings generated by the Iran war, outperforming their U.S. rivals. The windfall underscores the outsized advantage integrated European majors hold through proprietary trading operations during periods of extreme market dislocation. The results are expected to feature prominently in upcoming quarterly earnings disclosures.
Permian Oversupply Crushes Waha Prices; LNG Risk Underpins Henry Hub
NGI
Waha spot natural gas prices tumbled sharply on strong Permian Basin production, weakened LNG feed gas demand tied to maintenance outages, and soft shoulder-season weather across the region. Meanwhile, June NYMEX futures edged higher on the week as bulls looked to LNG supply risks and a separate story emerged around Northwest U.S. repricing pressure caused by plunging Canadian imports. The divergence between basin-level weakness and broader market support reflects the complex, regionalized nature of current natural gas fundamentals.
One-fifth of U.S. renewable diesel and SAF production was exported in 2H25
EIA
The United States exported nearly 50,000 barrels per day (b/d) of renewable diesel and other biofuels—a category which includes sustainable aviation fuel (SAF)—in the second half of 2025 (2H25), about 20% of the combined production for those fuels. About half of these exports went to Canada, with the rest mostly going to Europe.
Market Outlook
Crude markets opened the week in full risk-premium repricing mode, with Brent at $103.92 and WTI at $97.89 reflecting a decisive shift in trader positioning following Trump's rejection of Iran's peace response and active Israeli signaling that the conflict is far from resolved. The most important medium-term risk is the one Morgan Stanley has now quantified: the twin buffers of reduced Chinese imports and elevated U.S. export volumes are finite, and if Hormuz disruption persists beyond their exhaustion point, the market faces a supply shock with no obvious offset — a scenario that could drive prices well beyond current levels on very little additional news flow. GPS spoofing and erratic AIS data in the Persian Gulf are not background noise; they are operational signals that physical routing disruption is either underway or being actively prepared for, and traders should treat any further degradation in vessel tracking data as a leading indicator of price action. On the demand side, the renewable diesel and SAF export data — nearly 50,000 b/d shipped in 2H25 — is a reminder that refined product dynamics are shifting structurally, but this is a slow-moving factor that will not materially offset the acute geopolitical supply premium being priced today. The $4.75 billion trading windfall booked by BP, Shell, and TotalEnergies is a signal, not just a headline: integrated majors with proprietary books are positioned for continued volatility, and their desk activity will amplify price swings in both directions as news flow remains binary around diplomatic outcomes. Key catalysts to watch this week include any formal Iranian or U.S. diplomatic statement, Baker Hughes rig count data for signs of U.S. supply response, Chinese crude import figures, and U.S. export terminal flow data — collectively, these will determine whether the current $97-$104 range holds or breaks decisively higher.
