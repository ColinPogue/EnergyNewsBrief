G7 Eyes Emergency SPR Release as Iran War Rattles Markets
Financial Times / OilPrice
G7 finance ministers are set to hold an emergency call with the IEA on Monday to coordinate a strategic petroleum reserve release in response to the price shock triggered by the Iran conflict, according to the Financial Times. Market Outlook
The Strait of Hormuz closure is the most significant supply disruption event since the 2022 Ukraine invasion and markets are repricing accordingly, with Brent at $109.22 and WTI at $106.68 reflecting a war premium that has yet to fully account for worst-case scenarios. The drone attack on a Saudi Aramco oilfield producing approximately 1 million bpd introduces direct upside risk to production capacity; if Aramco confirms output curtailment, JPMorgan's warning of further Brent spikes becomes the base case, not the tail risk. On the demand-destruction side, Trump's framing of energy price pain as acceptable collateral damage signals limited political appetite for emergency domestic relief, reducing the probability of a swift diplomatic off-ramp. The G7 emergency SPR coordination — the first intervention of this scale since 2022 — is the key near-term bearish catalyst to monitor; a credible, large-volume release could inject 60-100 million barrels into the market and engineer a $5-8 correction from current levels, but execution risk is high and market skepticism of SPR efficacy at these price levels is warranted. Natural gas at $3.40 is riding geopolitical coattails rather than domestic fundamentals, and traders should treat the NG bid as fragile — any de-escalation headline or SPR-driven oil selloff could unwind the gas move quickly, with Waha basis weakness already telegraphing the structural softness beneath the surface. Key risk matrix for the week: watch for Aramco damage assessment, G7 SPR volume and timeline, any Hormuz reopening signals, and whether Gulf producers formally declare force majeure on export contracts — that last trigger would be the most structurally bullish development this market has seen in decades.
