Oil Drops 4% as US-Iran Deal Hopes Offset Hormuz Closure
OilPrice / CNBC / Upstream Online
WTI fell 4.3% to $89.83 and Brent dropped 3.7% to $95.94 early Wednesday as traders priced in the possibility of a US-Iran peace agreement, even as the Strait of Hormuz remained closed following fresh US strikes and Iran vowed revenge. ADNOC sent a second LNG carrier through Hormuz in 'dark mode' with AIS transponders disabled, underlining the severe operational risks in the waterway. Markets remain on edge over reports that Iran may seek to impose transit fees on Hormuz shipping as part of any eventual deal.
ECB Warns Trump's Iran War Risks Triggering Global Financial Crisis
Financial Times
ECB Vice-President Luis de Guindos cautioned that the US-Iran conflict, combined with Washington's volatile trade policies and reduced international cooperation, poses a serious threat to global financial stability. The warning adds a macro-financial dimension to the geopolitical risk premium already embedded in crude markets. For energy traders, any escalation that deepens the financial contagion scenario could amplify demand destruction fears alongside supply disruption risks.
BP Chairman Manifold Ousted Over Boardroom Bullying Scandal
CNBC / Financial Times / World Oil
BP's board unanimously removed Chairman Albert Manifold with immediate effect following findings of serious conduct concerns, including alleged bullying behavior and improper use of personal devices, piling fresh governance pressure on CEO Meg O'Neill. Manifold has publicly disputed the accusations, intensifying scrutiny on BP's leadership at a critical strategic juncture for the company. The removal is the latest in a series of boardroom upheavals at BP as it navigates its energy transition strategy.
Natural Gas Spot Prices Surge on Summer Heat and Rising LNG Demand
NGI
US natural gas spot prices rallied Tuesday on forecasts for the onset of summer heat across the Lower 48 and expectations of increasing LNG feedgas demand, while futures gave back gains late in the session as the expiring June contract faced selling pressure. NGI projects the EIA's weekly storage report will show a 92 Bcf injection for the week ended May 22, below historical norms despite a current storage surplus. The Iran conflict continues to underpin LNG market sentiment, as a prolonged Hormuz closure would reshape global LNG trade flows.
Aramco Exits Malaysia Refining Complex as Petronas Asserts Control
Rigzone
Saudi Aramco has divested its stake in a Malaysian refining and petrochemical complex, with state-owned Petronas citing the need for 'operational agility' over assets of strategic national significance amid volatile global energy markets. The exit marks a notable retreat by Aramco from a major downstream partnership in Southeast Asia and raises questions about the kingdom's broader downstream internationalization strategy. Traders will watch whether Petronas seeks alternative partners or consolidates full operational control.
Market Outlook
Crude markets are navigating a dangerous paradox: the Strait of Hormuz remains physically closed with ADNOC running tankers in dark mode, yet WTI has shed over 4% on the mere possibility of a US-Iran diplomatic resolution — underscoring just how much supply-disruption premium had been baked in and how quickly it can unwind on headline risk. The gap between market pricing and ground-level operational reality is now the central tension for traders; any breakdown in deal talks or Iranian escalation — Tehran has already vowed revenge and is reportedly eyeing Hormuz transit fees as a negotiating lever — could trigger a violent snapback toward the recent highs. The ECB's explicit warning that the Iran conflict risks triggering a global financial crisis adds a demand-destruction layer that bears watching: if macro contagion fears intensify alongside a prolonged supply disruption, the usual bullish supply-shock playbook gets complicated by the specter of cratering global demand. On the supply side, Aramco's exit from its Malaysian refining complex is a quiet signal of strategic repositioning by the Saudis, and traders should monitor whether downstream partnership unraveling becomes a broader theme as volatile markets pressure international energy JVs. Natural gas is telling a different story — the $3.00 print on heat forecasts and LNG demand optimism is notable, and a prolonged Hormuz closure would fundamentally reshape global LNG trade flows, potentially creating a sustained structural bid under US LNG prices. Thursday's EIA storage report is the next hard data point; a print near the projected 92 Bcf injection that confirms below-normal injections despite the storage surplus could validate the gas bull case, while crude traders should stay glued to any diplomatic signals out of Washington and Tehran.
