Top Stories
First Crude Tanker Clears Hormuz as U.S. Blockade Bites
OilPrice / CNBC / Upstream Online
A Pakistan-flagged Aframax tanker became the first vessel to ship crude through the Strait of Hormuz since the U.S. blockade began Monday, carrying an estimated 450,000 barrels en route to Karachi. The transit marks a critical test of chokepoint flow, with the IEA warning Middle Eastern producers could take up to two years to restore output to pre-war levels. Trump faces escalating tanker diplomacy challenges stretching from Cuba to the Persian Gulf as markets weigh supply disruption risks.
Biodiesel Price Drops Below Diesel as Iran War Squeezes Oil
Financial Times
The Iran war-driven crude oil squeeze has pushed biodiesel prices below conventional diesel for the first time, triggering a scramble among European buyers to lock in alternative fuel supplies. The price inversion signals a structural demand shift toward substitutes as traditional oil supply chains face sustained disruption. For traders, the spread collapse opens new arbitrage dynamics across diesel, biodiesel, and feedstock markets.
White House Pushes More U.S. Drilling to Cap Surging Oil Prices
Upstream Online / Financial Times
The White House is calling on domestic producers to accelerate drilling activity as crude prices surge amid the Middle East conflict, framing increased output as both an economic and national security imperative. The push underscores Washington's strategy of leveraging U.S. energy export capacity to offset geopolitical supply shocks. The Financial Times notes that America's net-exporter status increasingly frees U.S. foreign policy from the constraints that historically limited aggressive action during energy crises.
EIA: U.S. LNG Exports to Surge 30% by 2027
EIA
The EIA's latest Short-Term Energy Outlook projects U.S. LNG exports will grow nearly 30% by end-2027 as five new liquefaction projects ramp up operations, with pipeline exports to Mexico also rising. Net natural gas exports are forecast to climb 18% to 18.7 billion cubic feet per day, cementing the U.S. as the world's dominant LNG supplier. The outlook provides a bullish structural backdrop for Henry Hub prices as export demand absorbs domestic supply.

Spot natural gas prices inched higher on Thursday amid hints of regional demand bumps, though gains were mostly modest and came off of low levels as buyers focused on bargains.
Market Outlook
WTI's 7.26% single-session collapse to $87.86 is the defining move, but traders should resist reading this as a clean risk-off signal — the underlying supply picture remains structurally impaired, with the IEA flagging a potential two-year recovery timeline for Middle Eastern output and only one Aframax carrying 450,000 barrels having cleared Hormuz since the blockade began Monday. The steep WTI contango — $91.30 in May 2026 rolling to $78.94 by September — tells a bearish medium-term demand story, likely pricing in economic slowdown and the White House's accelerated domestic drilling push, which threatens to add U.S. barrels into an already uncertain demand environment. The biodiesel-diesel price inversion in Europe is a critical watch: structural demand substitution, if it accelerates, removes a floor that traders have historically relied on during geopolitical supply squeezes, and the arbitrage dynamics across diesel, biodiesel, and feedstock markets could become a primary driver of refined product spreads over the next quarter. On the bullish side, any deterioration in Hormuz transit reliability — the single tanker test is far from proof of normalized flow — could reverse today's move violently and push Brent back above $100, particularly if Trump's tanker diplomacy in the Persian Gulf and Cuba simultaneously faces breakdown. Natural gas at $2.68 remains the relative bright spot with a credible structural bull case: 30% LNG export growth by 2027 and rising pipeline exports to Mexico will tighten the supply-demand balance materially, but the near-term catalyst is thin, and traders should size positions accordingly until injection season data clarifies storage trajectory. Key risks to monitor: Hormuz transit volume data in the next 48-72 hours, any White House drilling incentive announcements, EIA weekly storage figures for natural gas, and macro demand signals from China and Europe that could validate or invalidate the contango curve's pessimistic demand assumption.
