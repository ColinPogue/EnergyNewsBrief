Top Stories
Brent Slides Below $76 as Hormuz Reopens, Iran Deal Holds
CNBC / Rigzone
Brent crude fell below $76 per barrel — its lowest level since before the US-Iran conflict — as tankers resumed transiting the Strait of Hormuz following an interim US-Iran peace deal, flooding the market with additional supply. More than 11,000 seafarers stranded in the Persian Gulf are now being evacuated through Hormuz in a coordinated plan backed by both Iran and the US. President Trump used the price decline to intensify pressure on oil companies to lower gasoline prices at the pump.
Qatar LNG Output to Normalize in Weeks; US-Iran Hotline Key
Financial Times / OilPrice
Qatar's Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman al-Thani told the Financial Times that LNG production will return to normal within weeks, excluding one damaged facility, as QatarEnergy mobilizes operations. He also emphasized that a US-Iran communications hotline was essential to securing the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, through which a significant share of global LNG trade flows.
UAE Exit Shrinks OPEC+ Production Share and Spare Capacity
EIA
The UAE formally left OPEC on May 1, 2026, reducing the group's collective share of global crude oil production and spare capacity — a significant structural shift for a cartel that has historically wielded outsized pricing influence. The departure narrows OPEC+'s ability to manage supply swings and could complicate coordinated output decisions at a time when markets are already digesting geopolitical volatility from the US-Iran situation.
Asian Refiners Pull Back on Mideast Crude After Buying Spree
Rigzone
Asian refiners have stepped back from aggressive crude purchasing after a three-week buying spree of Middle Eastern barrels, with major oil companies and traders absorbing the surplus volumes. The slowdown suggests near-term demand from the region's top buyers is cooling, adding downward pressure on Mideast crude differentials and potentially widening the discount on grades competing for Asian placement.
Natural Gas Futures Slip as Cool Forecasts, Strong Output Weigh
NGI
July NYMEX natural gas futures declined Tuesday after cooler near-term weather forecasts trimmed cooling degree day expectations and reduced power burn projections, while robust production levels and expectations for adequate storage reinforced bearish sentiment. Cash prices were mixed, with Florida and Texas markets posting gains on regional heat demand, though sharp declines at several Southeast hubs offset those moves at the national level.
Market Outlook
Crude markets are repricing a geopolitical risk premium that built rapidly during the US-Iran conflict, and the pace of that unwind will depend on how durable the Hormuz reopening proves to be and whether OPEC+ can fill the coordination void left by the UAE's formal departure on May 1. Brent at $75.92 and WTI at $72.13 are already testing technically significant support levels, and the absence of a bullish US inventory catalyst — with EIA data currently unavailable — removes a potential floor for prices in the near term. The UAE exit from OPEC is a structural bearish development that markets may be underweighting: it narrows the cartel's spare capacity buffer to roughly 3–4 mb/d and complicates any coordinated supply response if prices continue to slide toward the low $70s. Asian refiner pullback after a three-week Mideast buying spree signals that the demand side is not positioned to absorb incremental Hormuz supply, and widening Mideast crude differentials could compress margins for regional refiners further, feeding back into lower spot demand. On natural gas, the $3.21 print looks fragile — Qatar LNG normalization within weeks adds supply to an already well-stocked global market, and cooler US weather forecasts reduce the near-term power burn catalyst that had been supporting prices above $3.00. Traders should watch the $75 Brent level and $70–71 WTI zone as key downside thresholds, monitor any OPEC+ emergency meeting signals in response to the UAE exit, and track Hormuz transit volumes daily for any signs of deal deterioration that could rapidly reverse the risk premium unwind.
