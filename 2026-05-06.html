Top Stories
OPEC Output Hits 36-Year Low as Iran War Chokes Gulf Supply
World Oil / Rigzone
OPEC production has fallen to its lowest level in 36 years as the ongoing Iran conflict continues to disrupt Gulf supply chains, with the Strait of Hormuz remaining closed for nearly nine weeks. Oil prices dropped Wednesday on reports of US-Iran peace negotiations that raised hopes for a Hormuz reopening, easing the geopolitical risk premium. Shipowners, however, remain cautious about transiting the strait despite peace deal speculation, keeping freight and insurance costs elevated.
US Fuel Exports Hit Record as Hormuz Closure Reshapes Global Flows
Financial Times / OilPrice
The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has triggered a surge in Asian and European buying of US petroleum exports, pushing US fuel exports to record levels in a windfall for American oil companies. Simultaneously, Fujairah on the UAE's east coast has emerged as the pivotal crude and bunkering hub outside the strait, handling rerouted flows from the Gulf. US shale producers, however, are showing restraint in ramping output despite elevated prices, given limited slack in the system.
Iran War Sparks Jet Fuel Shortage Fears; US Airline Costs Surge 56%
CNBC
US airlines spent 56.4% more on jet fuel in March compared to February, according to government data, as the Iran war-driven supply disruption ripples through refined product markets. Oil executives are warning that actual fuel shortages could materialize in parts of Asia and Europe ahead of the summer travel season if the crisis persists. The sharp cost spike underscores the downstream impact of Gulf supply constraints on aviation and consumer-facing energy markets.
Nat Gas Futures Slide on Iran Peace Talks, LNG Feed Gas Lull
NGI
Natural gas futures fell for a second consecutive session Wednesday, pressured by US-Iran peace talk optimism that weighed on the broader energy complex and a month-long decline in LNG feed gas demand. Cash prices also softened as weather forecasts indicated a short-lived cold snap that would fade before mid-May, removing near-term support. On the supply side, federal regulators approved feed gas introduction to Cheniere's Train 6 cold-end, signaling incremental LNG demand growth in coming weeks from the Stage 3 expansion.
US Rules Out Further SPR Releases Despite Ongoing Supply Crisis
Upstream Online
A US energy official confirmed the government is not considering additional Strategic Petroleum Reserve releases to counter the supply disruption caused by the Iran conflict and Hormuz closure. The decision leaves markets reliant on rerouted trade flows and non-Gulf producers to absorb the supply gap, with no near-term policy buffer from Washington. The stance adds upside risk to crude prices if the Hormuz situation remains unresolved and peace negotiations stall.
Market Outlook
The dominant market theme today is a sharp peace-premium unwind, with Brent dropping 7.7% and WTI shedding 7.1% on credible signals that US-Iran negotiations could resolve the nine-week Strait of Hormuz closure — the most disruptive supply event in over three decades. The bear case is now in play: if diplomacy holds, Gulf barrels begin rerouting back through Hormuz, and the geopolitical risk premium — which had been the primary price support pillar — continues to deflate, Brent could test $98-$99 within sessions, with WTI threatening a move below $93. However, the no-SPR-release confirmation from Washington materially caps downside, as markets have no policy buffer to absorb any re-escalation; this asymmetry keeps the risk skew tilted toward violent upside reversals on any diplomatic setback. On the demand side, the 56.4% month-over-month surge in US airline jet fuel costs and executive warnings of potential summer fuel shortages in Asia and Europe underscore that downstream stress has not abated — refinery margins and product cracks will be the next pressure point to watch even if crude softens. US fuel export volumes hitting record highs and Fujairah's emergence as the new Gulf bunkering hub are structural flow shifts that will not unwind quickly regardless of a Hormuz resolution, providing a floor for US benchmark differentials. Traders should watch the credibility and timeline of the US-Iran peace framework, any resumption of Hormuz transits by major shipowners — who remain cautious on freight and insurance despite talks — and the pace of LNG feed gas recovery at Cheniere Stage 3 for near-term natural gas direction.
