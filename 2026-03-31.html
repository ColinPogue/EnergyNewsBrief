Top Stories
Iran Drone Hits Kuwait Tanker, Hormuz Closure Threatens $200 Oil
Rigzone / OilPrice / Upstream Online
An Iranian drone struck a fully laden Kuwaiti oil tanker off Dubai, one of the most significant vessel attacks since Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz on March 2, pushing crude prices sharply higher. Energy consultant Fereidun Fesharaki warned oil could spike to $200 per barrel or beyond if the near-closure persists another six weeks, with roughly 100 million barrels of oil in transit affected weekly. India has confirmed at least 28 ships — carrying crude, LPG, and LNG — are currently stranded near the strait, underscoring the deepening supply disruption across Asian import markets.
Trump's Iran War Remarks Spark Choppy Oil Trading; G-7 Eyes Stability
CNBC / World Oil / Upstream Online
Oil prices traded erratically Tuesday after President Trump signaled openness to ending military hostilities with Iran even if the Strait of Hormuz remains largely shut, creating uncertainty over the conflict's trajectory. The G-7 separately signaled readiness to intervene to stabilize oil markets amid the ongoing Iran conflict, while the IEA launched a dedicated energy crisis summary tracking member-state responses to the Middle East war. Traders are closely watching diplomatic signals alongside physical supply disruptions as the geopolitical risk premium remains elevated.
Cyclone Hammers Chevron's Wheatstone LNG, Tightening Global Gas Supply
OilPrice / NGI
Chevron's Wheatstone LNG facility in Western Australia has sustained extensive cyclone damage, with both liquefaction trains offline and multiple air-cooled heat exchangers destroyed, adding a fresh supply shock to an already war-strained global LNG market. Global natural gas prices moved higher Monday as the Australian outage compounded ongoing Middle East disruptions, though U.S. natural gas futures fell — May Nymex settled down 13.8 cents at $2.887/MMBtu — as soft domestic shoulder-season demand overwhelmed the international supply tightening. Asian buyers already scrambling to replace Gulf cargoes now face an additional shortfall from a key Pacific Basin supplier.
Asia Pivots to Coal as Iran War Chokes Gulf Gas Supplies
Financial Times / CNBC
Asian nations are accelerating coal consumption as the Iran war severely curtails natural gas flows through the Strait of Hormuz, with governments setting aside environmental commitments to keep power grids stable. South Korea, which sources 94% of its energy needs externally and roughly 72% of its crude from the Middle East, proposed a $17 billion supplementary budget to cushion surging energy costs for households and industry. The dual demand signal — coal up, gas constrained — is reshaping near-term Asian commodity flows and freight markets.
Natural gas liquids exports reached record highs in 2025
EIA
Natural gas liquids (NGL) exports reached 3.1 million barrels per day (b/d) in 2025, growing 7% from the previous year. These fuels are primarily extracted from the natural gas stream. NGL plant production has increased every year since 2005, driven by higher production of NGLs and more global demand for NGLs, especially as petrochemical feedstocks.
Market Outlook
The dominant near-term tension is the collision between a severely disrupted physical oil market and a diplomatically fluid geopolitical narrative — Brent's $5.37 single-day drop to $107.51 illustrates how quickly the war premium can deflate on a single Trump headline, even as the structural supply shock remains intact with 100 million barrels weekly in transit affected and 28 ships stranded near Hormuz. Fereidun Fesharaki's $200/bbl warning is not a base case but is a credible tail risk if the near-closure extends another six weeks, and traders should maintain asymmetric upside exposure in Brent until a verifiable diplomatic off-ramp materializes. The WTI outperformance at $104.29 — gaining 1.44% on a day Brent lost 4.76% — signals the market is beginning to reprice U.S. barrels as a supply alternative, a dynamic reinforced by record NGL exports of 3.1 Mb/d and a structurally productive domestic upstream. Natural gas at $2.84 is the most mispriced asset in the complex relative to global fundamentals: Wheatstone's full outage removes a key Pacific Basin supply node precisely when Asian buyers are already pivoting to coal and scrambling for replacement LNG, yet U.S. shoulder-season demand is keeping the prompt contract anchored below $3.00. The macro wildcard is the G-7's signaled readiness to intervene for market stability — coordinated SPR releases or diplomatic pressure could cap Brent well short of $120, while a breakdown in Iran talks or a second vessel strike near Hormuz would invalidate the current de-escalation pricing immediately. Key data to watch: IEA crisis summary updates, any movement on stranded vessel clearances, Wheatstone repair timeline from Chevron, and the next U.S. inventory report for evidence of domestic draw acceleration.
