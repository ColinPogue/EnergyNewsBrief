Top Stories
Hormuz Ceasefire Struck, But Tanker Transit Remains Weeks Away
World Oil / CNBC / OilPrice
A ceasefire deal has been reached that nominally reopens the Strait of Hormuz following the February 28 military action, but shipping experts warn meaningful tanker traffic normalization is still weeks, if not months, away. Chinese tankers linked to Cosco tested the chokepoint Thursday, accelerating toward the exit before halting at the entrance, signaling vessel owners are probing the terms rather than committing to full transit. The disruption has already triggered a sharp spike in global crude and product prices, emergency reserve releases, and a wholesale restructuring of trade flows away from Middle Eastern supply.
US Oil and Jet Fuel Exports Hit Records as Iran War Reshapes Trade
Financial Times
An 'armada' of Asian tankers is heading to American ports as the Iran war triggers a global scramble to replace disrupted Middle Eastern supply, pushing US crude exports toward record highs. The UK has simultaneously become the top destination for US jet fuel, with British aviation rapidly pivoting away from Middle East-sourced product. The shift marks a structural realignment in global energy trade flows with lasting implications for crude differentials, freight rates, and US export terminal utilization.
Sanctioned Russian Arctic LNG Offered to Asia at 40% Discount
OilPrice
Russia is marketing cargoes from the sanctioned Novatek Arctic LNG 2 facility to South Asian buyers at discounts of up to 40% to spot prices, according to Bloomberg, as the facility seeks outlets blocked by US, EU, and UK sanctions. The deep discounts come as global LNG spot prices have surged due to the Hormuz-driven supply disruption, making even heavily discounted Russian volumes commercially attractive to price-sensitive Asian buyers. The development raises compliance and secondary-sanctions risk concerns for any counterparties considering the cargoes.

Market Outlook
The crude complex is repricing aggressively around a single dominant theme: the Strait of Hormuz remains functionally closed for commercial tanker traffic despite the ceasefire, and the market is now pricing for a multi-week, potentially multi-month disruption. WTI at $99.56 and Brent at $98.68 are both knocking on the $100 door, and with no credible near-term restoration of Hormuz flows — evidenced by Cosco vessels probing the entrance but refusing to commit — the path of least resistance remains higher. The structural trade-flow realignment underway is not a transient spike: record US crude and jet fuel exports, an armada of Asian tankers rerouting to American ports, and the UK pivoting to US jet fuel supply all point to a durable tightening of non-Middle Eastern export capacity that will support differentials and keep freight rates elevated for months. On the downside, the key macro risk is demand destruction — crude above $100 sustained over weeks historically triggers IEA emergency reserve releases at scale, coordinated demand-side responses, and eventual recession fears that can reverse geopolitical premia sharply; the emergency releases already triggered per today's reporting are an early warning sign. The sanctioned Russian Arctic LNG 2 discount offering at 40% below spot introduces a secondary risk: if Asian buyers absorb those volumes and reduce their LNG spot market exposure, it could cap natural gas upside and complicate the bullish LNG narrative built on Hormuz disruption. Traders should watch three catalysts closely in coming sessions: confirmed commercial tanker transits through Hormuz, the next US crude inventory report for evidence of export-driven domestic tightening, and any IEA or SPR release announcement that could mechanically cap the rally below $105 Brent.
