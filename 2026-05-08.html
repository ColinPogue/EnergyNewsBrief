Top Stories
US Fires on Iran Tankers as Hormuz Ceasefire Frays
CNBC / OilPrice / Upstream Online
The U.S. struck two Iran-flagged oil tankers attempting to skirt a naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz, while the UAE came under fresh missile attack, sending oil prices higher and deepening doubts about the durability of any ceasefire. Markets remain confused by conflicting signals from ongoing U.S.-Iran negotiations even as kinetic exchanges continue, with ICE Brent on track for a 7% weekly loss near $101/bbl. The fragile security situation in the world's most critical oil chokepoint is keeping traders on high alert for further escalation.
Iraq Sanctions: Deputy Oil Minister Aided Iran Crude Diversion
OilPrice
The U.S. sanctioned Iraq's Deputy Oil Minister Ali Maarij al-Bahadly on Thursday, alleging he facilitated the blending and opaque sale of Iraqi crude mixed with Iranian oil to benefit Tehran and circumvent sanctions. Iraq's Oil Ministry flatly denied the accusations, calling them baseless. The move escalates Washington's pressure campaign on Iranian oil revenues and adds a new layer of geopolitical risk to Iraqi crude supply reliability.
Aramco, ADNOC Quietly Resume Limited Hormuz Crude Exports
World Oil
Saudi Aramco and ADNOC have cautiously resumed limited crude oil exports through the Strait of Hormuz, signaling a tentative easing of the supply blockage that has roiled global oil markets. The move is being watched closely by traders as a potential indicator of whether the world's most critical chokepoint can sustain even partial throughput amid ongoing hostilities. Any meaningful restoration of Gulf crude flows would be a significant bearish catalyst against an otherwise tight supply backdrop.
Cheniere Sees Iran War Driving Aggressive LNG Contracting Surge
NGI
Cheniere Energy management described themselves as 'astounded' by current LNG prices and expects the Iran conflict to accelerate contracting for U.S. LNG as global buyers scramble to secure reliable supply away from damaged Middle East infrastructure. Gulf Coast LNG feed gas demand has been temporarily suppressed by seasonal maintenance, but a sharp rebound is expected once work concludes, with global supply tightness already evident. Mexico's parallel announcement of a $7.8 billion natural gas grid expansion through 2030, tied to 13 new power plants, adds another demand signal for North American gas markets.
One-fifth of U.S. renewable diesel and SAF production was exported in 2H25
EIA
The United States exported nearly 50,000 barrels per day (b/d) of renewable diesel and other biofuels—a category which includes sustainable aviation fuel (SAF)—in the second half of 2025 (2H25), about 20% of the combined production for those fuels. About half of these exports went to Canada, with the rest mostly going to Europe.
Market Outlook
The Hormuz risk premium is back in play with a vengeance after US forces struck two Iran-flagged tankers attempting to breach the naval blockade, and fresh missile attacks on the UAE underscore that the security situation around the world's most critical oil chokepoint is deteriorating, not stabilizing. Brent's hold above $101 is the key near-term test — the 7% weekly drawdown heading into today's session shows how quickly geopolitical risk can be priced out, and any credible ceasefire signal or confirmed scaling of Aramco/ADNOC export volumes through Hormuz could reassert that bearish pressure violently. The Iraq Deputy Oil Minister sanctions add a compounding risk layer: if markets begin pricing reliability discounts into Basra Heavy flows on top of Hormuz uncertainty, the effective supply picture tightens materially beyond what headline OPEC+ figures suggest. On the demand side, the WTI futures curve's steep contango collapse from $95.39 in Jun 2026 to $82.71 by Oct 2026 tells traders the market believes current tightness is transient — that spread is the key instrument to monitor as Hormuz throughput data clarifies. Natural gas at $2.75 looks like a slow-burn setup: the Iran conflict is accelerating LNG contracting, Mexico's grid buildout is a durable 5-year demand tailwind, and the feed gas maintenance suppression is temporary — a move through $2.90 on the resumption of full LNG utilization is a high-conviction trade. Key risk to the entire complex remains a formal US-Iran diplomatic breakthrough, which would simultaneously release impounded Iranian barrels, reduce the Hormuz premium, and crater the LNG contracting urgency that is underpinning gas sentiment.
