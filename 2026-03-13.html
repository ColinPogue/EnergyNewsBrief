Top Stories
Hormuz Blockade Triggers Saudi Shut-In, Brent Surges Past $100
OilPrice / CNBC / Upstream Online
Saudi Arabia has cut oil output by roughly 2 million bpd — about 20% of its production — after shutting down the offshore Safaniya and Zuluf fields as Iran's effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz chokes Persian Gulf exports. Iran, despite losing most of its conventional naval fleet to U.S.-Israeli strikes since late February, is deploying asymmetric warfare tactics to maintain its stranglehold on the waterway through which 20% of global oil supply flows. Brent crude has topped $100 for the first time in years, and analysts warn no combination of SPR releases or U.S. drilling increases can offset a supply shock of this magnitude.
France, Italy Open Talks With Iran Over Hormuz Energy Passage
Financial Times / World Oil
France and Italy have entered direct negotiations with Tehran seeking safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz for energy shipments, as Iran's new supreme leader vows to keep the waterway shut. The diplomatic outreach underscores Europe's acute vulnerability to the supply disruption, with Russian sanction debates adding further complexity — Germany has publicly warned that any easing of Russian oil sanctions at this moment sends the wrong geopolitical signal.
Venture Global Reaches FID on CP2 Phase 2, Closes $8.6B Financing
NGI / Upstream Online
Venture Global has sanctioned the second phase of its CP2 LNG project in Cameron Parish, Louisiana, marking the first U.S. LNG export facility to reach FID in 2026, and has simultaneously closed $8.6 billion in project financing. The move positions Venture Global to surpass all rivals and claim the top spot among U.S. LNG exporters by total capacity once the project comes online.
U.S. Record Gas Output Hits 118.5 Bcf/d; Futures Slide on Spring Outlook
EIA / NGI
U.S. marketed natural gas production averaged a record 118.5 Bcf/d in 2025, up 5.3 Bcf/d year-on-year, with Appalachia, the Permian, and Haynesville accounting for 67% of total output and 81% of annual growth, per EIA's latest Natural Gas Monthly. Despite a brief cold shot expected early next week, April Nymex gas futures fell sharply at midday Friday as traders shifted focus toward a broader spring warming trend and loosening supply-demand balances.
U.S. Crude Stocks Build 4MM Barrels; Rig Count Ticks Up
Rigzone / OilPrice
U.S. commercial crude inventories rose by nearly 4 million barrels week-on-week to 443.1 million barrels as of March 6, according to EIA's weekly petroleum status report. The Baker Hughes rig count showed a slight recovery, with total active U.S. oil and gas rigs rising to 553, though that figure remains 39 rigs below year-ago levels, with the oil-directed count still 75 rigs lower than the same period last year.
Market Outlook
The dominant macro theme entering next week is unambiguously the Hormuz supply shock, and traders should treat the $100 Brent print not as a ceiling but as a potential launching pad if Iran's asymmetric naval campaign — deploying mines, drones, and fast-attack craft despite heavy conventional losses — continues to choke the 20-million-bpd waterway. Saudi Arabia's 2 million bpd shut-in at Safaniya and Zuluf is a hard supply loss; analysts are correct that no realistic SPR release cadence or U.S. drilling ramp — with the oil-directed rig count still 75 rigs below year-ago levels — can offset that magnitude of withdrawal in the near term. The Franco-Italian diplomatic channel to Tehran is the single most important variable to monitor: a credible passage agreement would likely trigger a sharp $4-6 corrective pullback in Brent, while a collapse in talks, or any escalation involving tanker seizures or mining of the strait's western approaches, could push Brent toward $108-$112. On the bearish side, the 4-million-barrel U.S. crude stock build to 443.1 million barrels introduces a domestic demand softness signal worth watching — if builds persist for a second consecutive week, it would suggest U.S. refinery throughput is not absorbing supply at the current pace and could widen the WTI-Brent spread further. Natural gas is trading its own story: with production at a record 118.5 Bcf/d and the market pivoting to spring, $3.12 is unlikely to hold as a floor unless the early-week cold shot surprises to the upside in degree-day demand, and traders should watch the $3.00 psychological level as the next meaningful support. The Venture Global CP2 Phase 2 FID is structurally constructive for Henry Hub on a 2-3 year horizon as LNG feed gas demand builds, but provides no near-term price support.
