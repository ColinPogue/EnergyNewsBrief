Top Stories
Iran War Chokes Hormuz, IEA Flags Record Inventory Draw
CNBC / Financial Times / World Oil / Upstream Online
The U.S.-Iran conflict, now in its third month, has locked down the Strait of Hormuz and driven oil prices above $100 per barrel, with the IEA warning of further price spikes as global inventories fall at their fastest recorded pace. An Iraqi supertanker was turned back near a U.S. blockade in the strait, illustrating the acute disruption to physical flows. Defense Secretary Hegseth confirmed Trump does not need Congressional authorization to resume strikes on Iran, keeping the geopolitical risk premium firmly elevated.
Iran Conflict Fundamentally Reshapes Global Oil Markets: Houlihan Lokey
Rigzone
J.P. Hanson, Managing Director and Global Head of Oil & Gas at Houlihan Lokey, says the Iran conflict has fundamentally altered the global energy landscape, with supply route disruptions and pricing dynamics now structurally different from pre-conflict norms. Russia, meanwhile, expects flat oil output in 2026 and only modest growth in subsequent years as Ukrainian drone attacks continue to degrade its energy infrastructure. Together, the two supply-side shocks are compressing available global crude volumes at a critical moment for inventory management.
Europe Seeks Storage Mandate Relief as Middle East War Squeezes LNG
OilPrice
EU gas industry lobby groups are pressing Brussels to ease the bloc's 90%-by-November storage refill targets, citing a severe LNG supply crunch stemming directly from the Middle East conflict. The war has removed a significant portion of global LNG supply, making the annual refill obligation increasingly difficult to meet within current market conditions. Failure to secure a waiver could force Europe into aggressive spot purchasing that would further tighten an already strained global gas market heading into winter.
Venture Global Adds Short-Term LNG Deals, Shares Surge on Profit Upgrade
NGI / Upstream Online
Venture Global signed a third five-year LNG supply agreement and expanded a contract inked in March, signaling a strategic push to capture near-term demand with shorter-duration deals rather than solely long-term commitments. The company's shares jumped sharply after it issued a significantly higher 2026 profit forecast, reflecting strong LNG pricing and growing portfolio utilization. The moves underscore rising U.S. LNG export momentum at a time when global buyers are scrambling to replace supply disrupted by the Middle East conflict.
The United States set record energy production in 2025, again
EIA
Total energy production in the United States increased to a new record of 107 quadrillion British thermal units (quads) in 2025, a 3.4% increase from the previous record set in 2024, according to new data in our Monthly Energy Review. Total production was driven by record-high production in natural gas, crude oil, natural gas plant liquids (NGPLs), and renewables.
Market Outlook
The dominant macro theme remains the Hormuz blockade and its compounding effect on global crude supply, with the IEA flagging inventory draws at the fastest pace ever recorded — a data point that argues against any near-term price retreat despite WTI's relatively subdued $102.37 close. Houlihan Lokey's assessment that the conflict has structurally repriced supply routes is a critical framing for medium-term positioning: this is not a spike to fade, but a potential regime shift in global energy pricing. The second supply-side shock — Russian output expected flat in 2026 with Ukrainian drone strikes continuing to degrade infrastructure — removes what has historically been an elastic swing supply source, further tightening the global balance. On the demand side, Europe's struggle to meet gas storage mandates represents a key upside risk for both LNG and, by extension, oil-linked contracts, as forced spot LNG buying at scale would absorb flexible supply and push buyers toward alternative energy sources. U.S. record production at 107 quads is the primary bearish counterweight, and traders should watch whether accelerating domestic output can meaningfully offset Hormuz-driven seaborne supply losses — the widening Brent-WTI spread of ~$5.80 suggests the market is already pricing in that domestic supply cannot fully substitute. Next key catalysts: any signal from Defense Secretary Hegseth on resumed Iran strikes, the EU Commission's response to the storage mandate waiver request, and the delayed U.S. crude inventory data, which — when released — could either validate or challenge the IEA's record draw narrative.
