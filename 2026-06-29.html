Top Stories
U.S.-Iran Pause Hostilities; Hormuz Shipping Resumes After Weekend Clashes
CNBC / Rigzone / OilPrice
The U.S. and Iran agreed to halt recent military exchanges and restore commercial vessel transit through the Strait of Hormuz, easing immediate fears of a prolonged supply disruption. Oil prices responded by pushing WTI back above $70, though analysts warn markets may be too optimistic about a swift, durable resolution. Rigzone reported that commercial traffic through Hormuz had already ebbed following attacks on two ships, underscoring the fragility of the current pause.
Middle East Crude Output Rebounds to 15 Million Bpd Post-Ceasefire
OilPrice / NGI
Middle East crude production has recovered to between 14.6 million and 15 million bpd earlier this month following the Iran-U.S. ceasefire, according to Rystad Energy, which projects a full return to pre-war levels by year-end. Nine empty LNG tankers have also massed off Qatar's Ras Laffan Industrial City, signaling an imminent restart of LNG loadings from the world's largest liquefaction complex. Together, the signals suggest a significant near-term supply recovery that could weigh on both crude and LNG spot prices.
Europe Faces 15-Year Low Winter Gas Storage Amid Refill Shortfall
Financial Times
EU gas storage facilities are not being refilled quickly enough to meet winter demand, putting the continent on track for the lowest pre-winter inventory levels in 15 years, according to the Financial Times. The shortfall increases Europe's vulnerability to supply disruptions and cold-weather demand spikes heading into Q4, a key risk for gas traders monitoring TTF and related benchmarks.
Drone Strike Cuts Output at Kazakhstan's Karachaganak Oil Field
World Oil
A drone attack has disrupted production at Kazakhstan's Karachaganak oil and gas condensate field, one of the largest in the country and a key contributor to regional export volumes. The incident adds to a string of geopolitical supply disruptions and is likely to draw attention from traders tracking Caspian Basin output and CPC pipeline flows.
Metered electricity demand in the New York ISO falls midday because of small-scale solar
EIA
An increase in electricity generation from small-scale solar in New York has decreased the midday demand for metered electricity, amid overall declining load in the state. The trend is particularly notable in the early spring (March and April), when solar generation has an outsized impact because demand is relatively low and conditions for solar generation are favorable.
Market Outlook
Markets are trading a ceasefire relief narrative today, but the durability of that trade is already in question. WTI's inability to post a clean close above $70 — settling at $69.86 despite a broadly constructive headline environment — is a red flag for bulls; the Hormuz pause is fragile by analysts' own admission, and Rigzone noted commercial traffic had already ebbed following attacks on two vessels before the ceasefire was announced. On the supply side, Rystad Energy's projection of Middle East output recovering to full pre-war levels by year-end introduces a significant structural headwind, with production already back in the 14.6–15 million bpd range — a volume that, combined with a resumption of Qatari LNG loadings, could overwhelm the current demand-side support. The Karachaganak drone strike in Kazakhstan adds a genuine upside risk tail, particularly for CPC pipeline watchers; any sustained disruption to that corridor would tighten the Caspian Basin supply picture and offer a counterweight to returning Middle East barrels. On the gas side, the European storage deficit is the most structurally important story in the complex right now — 15-year seasonal lows heading into Q4 create material vulnerability to any cold-weather demand event or further supply shock, and traders long TTF or U.S. LNG export proxies have a credible fundamental thesis to lean on. Key data points to watch next: Baker Hughes rig count (data currently unavailable but expected; any uptick in U.S. oil-directed rigs would add supply-side pressure), EIA storage figures, and any diplomatic signaling out of Vienna or Washington on the Iran nuclear file, which remains the single highest-impact binary risk in the crude complex.
