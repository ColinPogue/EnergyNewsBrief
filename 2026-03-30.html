Top Stories
Trump Threatens Iran Oil Sites as Hormuz Closure Reshapes Markets
World Oil / Rigzone / Financial Times
President Trump has threatened strikes on Iranian oil infrastructure, including Kharg Island, if the Strait of Hormuz is not reopened, escalating a conflict now in its fifth week. J.P. Morgan analysts warn the war has spread beyond the Persian Gulf, flagging a risk premium of up to $20/bbl on crude prices, with Brent already topping $115/bbl. An Iranian strike on a Kuwaiti power and desalination facility marks the latest infrastructure escalation, stoking fears of wider Gulf energy disruption.
Brent Surges on Houthi Missiles, Iran War Enters Week Five
CNBC / OilPrice
Oil prices climbed sharply Monday after Yemen's Houthis fired missiles at Israel, opening a new front in the U.S.- and Israeli-led conflict with Iran and pushing Brent toward a record monthly gain. South Korea is now weighing public driving restrictions — the first since the 1991 Gulf War — if Brent hits $120/bbl, while India warned of slower growth and a wider fiscal deficit as energy costs surge. Saudi Arabia is also struggling to price May-loading cargoes for Asian buyers as the war upends regional benchmarks and oil flows.
Golden Pass LNG Reaches First Production After Years of Delays
NGI / Upstream Online
Golden Pass LNG has achieved first production at its Texas export terminal, a milestone long delayed by contractor bankruptcy, setting the stage for increased Gulf Coast feed gas demand and new LNG supply volumes in coming weeks. The startup is expected to tighten supply at Gulf Coast natural gas price hubs as feed gas nominations ramp up. The development arrives as Middle East war volatility continues to pressure global LNG markets.
Russia Strikes Ukraine Gas Fields Amid Baltic Terminal Fires
Upstream Online / CNBC
Russia struck Ukrainian natural gas fields in a retaliatory move following fires at Baltic oil terminals, marking a fresh escalation in energy infrastructure attacks with direct implications for European supply security. Separately, a Russian tanker carrying 100,000 tons of crude arrived in Cuba after the Trump administration softened its approach to the U.S. naval blockade, signaling a potential shift in enforcement posture. Both developments underscore how energy assets are increasingly being used as instruments of geopolitical leverage.
Natural gas liquids exports reached record highs in 2025
EIA
Natural gas liquids (NGL) exports reached 3.1 million barrels per day (b/d) in 2025, growing 7% from the previous year. These fuels are primarily extracted from the natural gas stream. NGL plant production has increased every year since 2005, driven by higher production of NGLs and more global demand for NGLs, especially as petrochemical feedstocks.
Market Outlook
The crude complex is flashing contradictory signals — Brent's sharp 4.59% decline alongside WTI's 2.59% rally suggests the market is simultaneously unwinding a stretched geopolitical premium while repricing domestic U.S. supply risk, a split that will likely resolve violently once the Hormuz situation clarifies. Trump's explicit threat to strike Kharg Island — Iran's primary crude export terminal handling roughly 90% of its oil exports — represents perhaps the single highest-impact supply risk in the market today, and J.P. Morgan's $20/bbl risk premium estimate implies Brent could retrace toward $127/bbl if hostilities escalate further. South Korea's contemplation of public driving restrictions at $120/bbl Brent and India's fiscal deficit warnings are early demand-destruction signals that could cap the upside and add reflexive volatility if prices spike sharply. On the supply side, Russia's strikes on Ukrainian gas fields and the Baltic terminal fires add a European energy security layer that could tighten global LNG markets just as Golden Pass ramps up — partially offsetting the bearish natural gas impulse from new U.S. export capacity. Baker Hughes rig count data is unavailable today, removing a key near-term supply growth signal for U.S. producers, though NGL exports hitting a record 3.1 MMb/d in 2025 underscores that the U.S. production complex remains structurally expansionary. Traders should watch Hormuz shipping reports, any White House statement on Iran strike authorization, and the next EIA inventory print as the three catalysts most likely to define directional momentum through the end of the month.
