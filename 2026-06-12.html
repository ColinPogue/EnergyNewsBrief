Top Stories
US-Iran Hormuz Deal Sends Crude Below $85
CNBC / Rigzone / OilPrice
Oil prices fell to a four-month low Friday after Iranian state media reported a proposed interim peace deal that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with WTI dropping below $85/bbl. The potential agreement follows three and a half months of the worst oil supply disruption in history, during which roughly 13 million bpd of supply was blocked. Market buffers including Chinese strategic reserves and OPEC spare capacity have kept prices from spiking further, but traders are now watching whether a formal deal materializes or Tehran pushback derails the rally reversal.
Natural Gas Futures Fall Second Week Despite Peak Cooling Demand
NGI
Natural gas futures posted a second consecutive weekly decline even as cooling demand hit its highest levels year-to-date, with ample production and healthy storage levels overwhelming the bullish demand signal. Forward prices also slipped despite some of the season's hottest weather on record and forecasts calling for continued heat, particularly across the West. Cash prices in West Texas, the Rockies, and the Southwest edged modestly higher, but gains were insufficient to reverse the broader bearish trend driven by oversupply.
Exxon Eyes Woodside in Potential Major LNG Acquisition
World Oil
ExxonMobil is reportedly evaluating Australian LNG major Woodside Energy as a potential acquisition target, a move that would represent one of the largest energy deals in recent years and significantly expand Exxon's global LNG portfolio. A deal would give Exxon greater exposure to Asia-Pacific LNG markets at a time when long-term gas demand in the region remains robust. No formal offer has been confirmed, but the report is likely to draw close attention from LNG traders and rivals monitoring consolidation in the sector.
APA Acquires Savant to Bolster Alaska North Slope Position
Rigzone
APA Corporation announced the acquisition of Savant, securing control of strategic infrastructure adjacent to its eastern North Slope acreage in Alaska and strengthening its ability to execute a planned drilling program efficiently. The deal expands APA's footprint in a basin that has seen renewed operator interest amid elevated oil prices and US energy security priorities. Terms of the transaction were not disclosed in initial reports.
Largest wind farm in the United States slated to begin commercial operations
EIA
The SunZia Wind Project, the largest wind farm in the United States, is slated to begin commercial operations this month. The wind farm, located in New Mexico, has a total net summer generating capacity of 3,650 megawatts (MW) and is composed of 916 wind turbines.
Market Outlook
The dominant story heading into the weekend is the reported US-Iran interim agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which single-handedly erased months of geopolitical risk premium embedded in crude prices and drove WTI to $84.29 — a four-month low. The critical unknown is whether this deal formalizes or fractures: Iranian state media reporting is not a signed agreement, and Tehran hardliners have historically derailed diplomatic momentum at the final hour. If a formal deal materializes, the market must now grapple with the potential re-entry of roughly 13 million bpd that has been offline for three and a half months — a supply shock of historic proportions that Chinese strategic reserve drawdowns and OPEC spare capacity have only partially offset. The WTI futures curve, already in backwardation from $84.29 in July to $78.35 in November 2026, suggests the market is beginning to price supply normalization, and a confirmed deal could accelerate the curve flattening and push front-month toward the low $80s. On the natural gas side, the fundamental picture remains bearish despite record cooling demand — oversupply and strong storage are the dominant forces, and the $3.14 print reflects a market struggling to sustain a rally even under ideal demand conditions. Key macro risks to monitor: a deal breakdown driving a violent crude short-squeeze, any OPEC+ response to the prospective supply surge, and whether the SunZia wind project ramp-up meaningfully pressures Southwest gas cash prices over the coming weeks.
