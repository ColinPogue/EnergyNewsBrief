Top Stories
Trump's Iran Strike Deadline Jolts Oil, Gas Markets Higher
CNBC / OilPrice / World Oil
Oil prices and European TTF gas futures both surged Tuesday as President Trump reaffirmed a deadline threatening strikes on Iranian power plants and bridges if a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is not reached. The Dutch TTF May 2026 contract opened up 3% as traders priced in escalation risk across global energy markets. The standoff has already forced Qatar LNG tankers to turn back from the strait, tightening global LNG supply chains.
Missile Strike Hits Saudi Strategic Energy Hub, Damage Assessed
Upstream Online
A missile strike has sparked a fire at a key Saudi Arabian energy infrastructure hub, with authorities conducting an ongoing damage assessment. The attack marks a significant escalation in regional hostilities and raises immediate concerns about Saudi export capacity and crude supply continuity. Traders are closely monitoring the situation given Saudi Arabia's central role in global oil markets.
India Pivots to Venezuelan and Iranian Crude Amid Hormuz Disruption
OilPrice / CNBC
India is set to import as many as 12.51 million barrels of Venezuelan crude in April — the highest volume in six years — as Middle East supply disruptions from the Strait of Hormuz closure force refiners to diversify sourcing. Separately, India has resumed oil and gas imports from Iran after a seven-year hiatus, signaling a strategic rebalancing of energy relationships amid U.S.-Iran tensions. The dual pivot underscores how the Hormuz standoff is reshaping global crude trade flows in real time.
Global Infrastructure Race Accelerates to Bypass Strait of Hormuz
Financial Times
New pipeline, rail, and port infrastructure is being rapidly developed across the Middle East and beyond to route energy and food supplies around the Strait of Hormuz chokepoint, according to the Financial Times. The buildout reflects a structural shift in how governments and energy companies are assessing long-term supply chain risk following the current conflict. Success could meaningfully reduce the geopolitical leverage that Hormuz disruptions have historically exerted on global oil and gas prices.
Middle East Gulf Supplied 8% of U.S. Crude Imports in 2025
EIA
The U.S. imported an average of 490,000 barrels per day from the Middle East Gulf region in 2025, accounting for 8% of total crude imports, according to EIA data. Volumes consisted primarily of medium sour grades flowing into West Coast and Gulf Coast refineries from Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and the UAE. The data provides critical context for assessing U.S. supply exposure as the Hormuz standoff threatens regional export flows.
Market Outlook
The dual shock of Trump's Iran strike deadline and a confirmed missile strike on Saudi energy infrastructure has injected a meaningful and potentially durable geopolitical risk premium into crude, with WTI at $114.16 and Brent at $110.19 reflecting only the opening bid on escalation pricing. The Saudi situation is the more immediate wildcard — until the damage assessment confirms or rules out export capacity loss, traders should treat upside risk as asymmetric; any confirmation of prolonged outage capacity could push WTI toward the $118-$120 zone in short order. India's record 12.51 million barrel April import of Venezuelan crude and the resumption of Iranian oil imports after a seven-year hiatus signal that Hormuz disruption is already materially reshaping global trade flows, adding logistical cost and complexity that keeps a floor under prices even if diplomatic resolution emerges. The U.S. is not immune: EIA data confirms 490,000 b/d of Middle East Gulf crude imports in 2025, concentrated in medium sour grades critical to specific West Coast and Gulf Coast refinery configurations, meaning a prolonged disruption creates domestic feedstock risk that the market has not fully priced. On the bearish side, the accelerating infrastructure buildout to bypass Hormuz — new pipelines, rail, and port capacity — represents a structural medium-term headwind to geopolitical leverage, and any credible diplomatic breakthrough on the U.S.-Iran front would trigger a sharp unwind of the current risk premium. Key data and events to monitor: Saudi damage assessment details, any movement on U.S.-Iran negotiations ahead of the strike deadline, EIA weekly inventory data, and TTF price action as a leading indicator of how global LNG markets are absorbing the Qatar tanker disruptions.
